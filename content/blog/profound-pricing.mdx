If you are researching Profound pricing, the first thing to know is that Profound does not publish one. There is no public price list on its site, only a credit allowance and a "Get a Demo" button. So every dollar figure you will find, including the ones below, comes from third-party reviewers, and they do not fully agree. This guide reconciles those numbers tier by tier, explains why the vendor keeps them off its site, and works through whether Profound is worth the cost or whether a cheaper tool does the same job.

Here is why the numbers wobble. Because Profound publishes no standard pricing, each reviewer captures a slightly different snapshot, and the company renames and repackages tiers over time. One source lists a $99 Starter and $399 Growth; another lists a $499 "Lite" plan the others never mention; an older version of Profound's own blog described a $100 "Explorer" and a $500 "Growth" plan with rounder numbers. The enterprise floor alone gets reported as $1,000, $2,000, and $2,000-to-$5,000-plus, depending on who is writing. None of that means a source is lying. It means the only honest answer to "what does Profound cost" is a sourced range with a date on it, which is what this page gives you.

## Why Profound Hides Its Pricing

Profound's [own pricing page](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) shows no dollar amounts at all. It lists credit allowances, a hundred a month on the entry plan and four hundred on the next, and routes every real decision to a sales demo. There is no self-serve checkout for the higher tiers and no free trial to test the product first.

That is a deliberate enterprise sales motion, not an oversight. Gating price behind a demo lets a vendor quote each account differently, anchor to the value a Fortune 500 sees rather than a fixed number, and avoid handing competitors a target. It also means you cannot compare Profound to a cheaper tool on price alone without first sitting through a sales call.

The practical effect on your side is simple. Budget for a conversation, not a card swipe. If you need a tool you can buy today and switch off next month, the demo-gated model is friction before you have seen a single report. And because the public page now points everything toward "customized enterprise pricing," the self-serve Starter and Growth tiers that reviewers describe are getting harder to find and sign up for directly.

## The $99 Starter Plan Is a ChatGPT-Only Demo

The $99 entry tier is the number that makes Profound look affordable, and it is the one to be most careful with. Reviewers are consistent that Starter tracks ChatGPT and nothing else: no Perplexity, no Gemini, no Google AI Overviews. For a tool whose whole pitch is visibility across the AI engines, watching one of them is a narrow slice.

The limits stack up from there. Starter is commonly reported at around 50 prompts, a single seat, roughly a week of history, and no data exports. There is no free trial, so you cannot confirm any of this before you pay. In practice the $99 plan works as a guided demo of the dashboard, not a tool you would run a real program on.

That matters for budgeting because it moves the real entry point. If you need to see how you show up across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews, which is the actual job most teams are buying this for, you are starting at the $399 Growth tier, not $99. Quoting yourself the $99 number and then discovering it only watches ChatGPT is the most common pricing surprise with Profound.

## What $399 Growth Actually Buys You

Growth, at roughly $399 a month, is where Profound becomes a working multi-engine tracker. It adds Perplexity and Google AI Overviews to ChatGPT, lifts you to three seats, and turns on the data exports and competitive benchmarking that the Starter tier withholds. For a small team that needs real [share of voice across the major engines](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/how-to-track-ai-visibility), this is the plan reviewers point to.

The catch is what Growth does not do at volume. Its content generation is capped, commonly reported at three to six articles a month, so the "write" side of the tool is more of a sampler than a production line. Reviewers repeatedly describe Profound as monitoring-heavy and execution-light: strong at telling you where you stand, thin on doing something about it.

Factor that into the real cost. If Growth tells you what to fix but caps how much you can act on, you are likely pairing it with a separate content or SEO tool, and the true monthly spend is Profound plus that. A $399 line item that needs a second subscription to be useful is a different decision than $399 all-in, and it is worth doing that math before you commit. ## Enterprise: Custom Pricing, and What Pushes It to $2,000-$5,000+

Everything that makes Profound a category leader lives in the custom Enterprise tier. That is where you get coverage across 10-plus engines, full API access, the proprietary Prompt Volumes data, Agent Analytics, SOC 2 Type II, and a dedicated support SLA. It is also the tier with no public number at all.

The estimates that circulate disagree on the floor. One reviewer puts the entry around $1,000 a month, another around $2,000, and two more describe a $2,000-to-$5,000-plus range depending on scale and seats. The honest read is that Enterprise is genuinely custom, priced per account, and lands somewhere in the low four figures for most buyers and higher for large, multi-brand ones.

Two technical conditions can change the value before you ever discuss price. First, Profound's crawler analytics, the feature that shows which AI bots fetch your pages, is reported to require a supported content delivery network such as Cloudflare, Akamai, or AWS CloudFront. If your site is not on one, that headline feature may not work for you. Second, Profound surfaces server-level bot activity but does not natively tie AI visibility to real revenue in GA4, so attributing dollars to a citation still takes work outside the tool. Neither is a dealbreaker on its own, but both are the kind of thing you want to confirm in the demo, not discover after signing an annual contract.

## Is Profound Worth the Price?

For the right buyer, yes. Profound is the most established tool in the category: it [raised a $96 million round at a $1 billion valuation in early 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/24/exclusive-as-ai-threatens-search-profound-raises-96-million-to-help-brands-stay-visible/), serves more than 700 enterprise customers including roughly a tenth of the Fortune 500, and carries a [strong user rating on G2](https://www.g2.com/products/profound/reviews) and SOC 2 Type II compliance. If you are a large brand with a dedicated analyst, budget for market-intelligence depth, and a need for prompt-volume data competitors estimate rather than measure, the price buys something real.

For most other buyers, the math is harder to justify. One comparison of dozens of AI-search monitoring tools pegged the market average near $337 a month and put Profound well above it, and that is before the gaps: the entry tier watches one engine, the mid tier caps the content work, and the enterprise tier asks for a four-figure annual commitment with no trial. A useful discipline is cost per prompt rather than sticker price. A plan that looks affordable but tracks 50 prompts can cost more per question answered than a pricier plan that tracks hundreds, so divide before you decide.

The cleanest way to frame it is by buyer profile.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does Profound cost?**
Profound does not publish standard pricing. Third-party reviewers consistently report a $99 Starter plan (ChatGPT only), a $399 Growth plan (adding Perplexity and Google AI Overviews), and a custom Enterprise tier estimated at roughly $2,000 to $5,000 or more per month. A disputed $499 "Lite" plan also appears in some reviews. Confirm the current figure with Profound directly, since the sources disagree and the tiers change.

**Does Profound have a free trial?**
Not on the main tiers. Profound's pricing page routes to a sales demo rather than a self-serve signup, and reviewers report no free trial on the entry plans. You generally cannot test the product before committing, which is one reason a free reachability or visibility check elsewhere is a sensible first step.

**Is the $99 Starter plan worth it?**
Only as a look around the dashboard. The $99 Starter plan tracks ChatGPT and nothing else, with around 50 prompts and a week of history, so it cannot show your visibility across Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews. For real multi-engine tracking, the effective entry point is the $399 Growth plan.

**Why doesn't Profound publish its pricing?**
Because it sells through an enterprise motion. Demo-gating lets Profound quote each account against the value it sees rather than a fixed price, and its public page now shows only credit allowances and a "Get a Demo" path. The tradeoff for buyers is that you cannot compare on price without a sales call.

**Is Profound worth it for a small business?**
Usually not. Profound is built for large brands with a dedicated analyst and budget for market-intelligence depth. A small business or agency that needs execution and revenue tracking for under $400 a month is generally better served by a cheaper, more action-focused tool.
