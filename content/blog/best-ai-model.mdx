### Gemini 4 Carbon: The Top Score You Cannot Buy

Carbon leads 3 leaderboards, and Google's own AI Overview for "best AI model" lists it among the current leaders with no access caveat. Google's [launch post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-carbon/) says it will make Carbon "available to a subset of cyber defenders" and phase in more trusted partners through the Fairwind Program. Google says it wants to open Carbon to developers, businesses and consumers "as soon as possible", and gives no date. Today there is no public plan and no general API.

For a buyer, Carbon is not an option yet. Treat its scores as a preview of where Gemini is heading, and pick from the models you can sign up for.

### Preference, Independent Index, Agent Boards

Each board answers a different question, which is why the order changes.

Arena ranks models by blind human preference. It rewards answers people like, not answers that are correct. Carbon's 1528 is flagged preliminary and rests on 4,403 votes, against 11,889 for Argon and 11,415 for Opus 5.5.

Artificial Analysis runs every model itself, and the effort setting changes the result a lot. Opus 5.5 scores 59 at max effort and 52 at high. When Google launched Carbon it cited 59 on the index; the live page now shows 60, so even one board moves.

Mercor and Terminal-Bench test agents, so each score covers the model plus its tooling. Terminal-Bench lists the agent beside every result: Claude Code for the Claude models and Codex for the OpenAI models. Mercor's live board is newer than its own September 23 "best models" page, which predates the Argon and Carbon launches and still puts Opus 5.5 first.

Vendor tables are self-run. Anthropic's own [Opus 5.5 post](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5) warns that "benchmark margins have become a less reliable guide to real-world differences", and its Terminal-Bench 4.0 row compares Opus 5.5 at xhigh effort with GPT-6 Astra at high effort, as reported by OpenAI. Anthropic says each figure is that model's highest score, but the settings differ.

Benchmarks also expire. OpenAI [stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/) because of flawed tests and contamination, and now recommends SWE-bench Pro. A coding score from an older leaderboard may measure a test at least one lab has stopped reporting.

### What We Tested, and What We Only Cite

Every benchmark score on this page is cited from its owner with a date. We did not rerun any of them. The one dataset we produced ourselves at geotoolbox is a panel: on November 6, 2026 we put 8 model-choice questions to 5 AI engines and logged every source each one cited. The 5 were a logged-out ChatGPT scrape plus an API call, Gemini, Google AI Mode, Perplexity's Sonar API and a Claude agent with web search.

The engines cited 183 distinct domains. Of those, 152 appeared in only one engine, and only 15 were cited by 3 or more, almost all vendor documentation plus leaderboards: anthropic.com, openai.com, x.ai, Artificial Analysis, Arena, BenchLM and LLM Stats. Outside that shared core, the engines mostly disagree on what to cite.

## Best AI Model for Coding

For agentic coding, **Claude Opus 5.5** is the best LLM on the evidence that does not come from a vendor's own launch post, with Claude Sonnet 5.5 second and GPT-6.1 Sol the one to pick when cost matters.

On the official Terminal-Bench 4.0 board, Opus 5.5 scores 64.8% with Claude Code and Sonnet 5.5 61.8%. GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol tie at 58.2% with Codex, and Fable 5.1 follows at 57.9%. The board also lists what each run cost, and GPT-6.1 Sol's was a fraction of Opus 5.5's and Sonnet 5.5's.

Mercor's live [APEX-SWE board](https://www.mercor.com/apex/apex-swe-leaderboard/) agrees on the top 2: Opus 5.5 at 67.6% and Sonnet 5.5 at 66.4%, then Opus 5 at 63.7% and Fable 5.1 at 63.6% among the entries its chart shows. Mercor reports each model with the harness that scores it highest, here Terminus-2.

Open weights are close on this one benchmark. [Moonshot's model card](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro) reports 65.7% for Kimi K3.1 Pro on Terminal-Bench 4.0 using the Claude Code harness, and its table takes other models' scores from their blog posts or the official leaderboard, so it is a vendor figure like any other.

The coding tool wraps the model and changes the score, so it matters too. For deeper dives, see our [Kimi K3 vs Claude comparison](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/kimi-k3-vs-claude), the [GPT-6 Astra breakdown](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/gpt-6-astra) and the [Claude Fable 5.1 review](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-fable-5-1).

## Best AI Model for Writing, Research, and Long Documents

For knowledge work the boards split. Opus 5.5 tops [Artificial Analysis's GDPval-AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gdpval-aa) at 1866, within the margin of Kimi K3.1 Pro (1858) and Muse Wonder Full (1857); Gemini 4 Argon sits far down that board at 1631. Argon leads Mercor's professional-tasks board (82.4% to 73.5% for Opus 5.5) and sits above Opus on Arena. Test both on your own documents.

Context size is the one hard number here, and the top models are close. Claude Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1 and Sonnet 5.5 all take 1M tokens. [GPT-6 Astra](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-astra) has a 1,050,000-token window, of which up to 922,000 can be input and 128,000 output. Gemini 4 Argon offers a 1M unified input-output limit through the API, and Grok 4.7 stops at 500K.

Writing quality has no neutral score beyond preference votes, and Arena's votes put Gemini and Claude models on top. For research, the major apps each ship a research mode (ChatGPT's [plans page](https://chatgpt.com/pricing) lists deep research on every plan, for example). We found no independent head-to-head test of them, so we do not rank them. For broader comparisons of the apps themselves, see [Claude vs ChatGPT](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-vs-chatgpt), [Claude vs Gemini](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-vs-gemini) and [Gemini vs ChatGPT](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/gemini-vs-chatgpt).

## How to Choose for Your Own Work

A leaderboard shortlists models. Your own tasks decide.

1. Check each vendor's data terms first if you handle client or customer data.
2. Shortlist 3 models that rank in the top 5 on at least 2 boards for your kind of work.
3. Match the settings. Compare scores at the same effort level and with the same tool, because both move results.
4. Run 20 to 50 of your own tasks through the tool you will use: the API, Claude Code, Codex, or the chat app.
5. Score whether the answer is correct, what the run cost, and how long it took.
6. Re-test when a vendor ships. Google released Carbon about 2 weeks after Argon, so a shortlist can be out of date within a month.

For a primer on why models behave differently on the same prompt, see [how AI models think](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/how-ai-models-think).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is the best AI model right now?

It depends on the job. Claude Opus 5.5 is the pick for coding, Gemini 4 Argon for Arena and agent scores among models you can use, and GPT-6.1 Sol for value per task. Gemini 4 Carbon scores higher on 3 boards but is not available yet.

### Is Gemini 4 Carbon available to the public?

No. Google says Carbon is available to a subset of cyber defenders and will phase in more trusted partners through its Fairwind Program. It has not published a date for wider access, although Arena and Artificial Analysis already list it.

### What are the major AI model families?

The major families are OpenAI's GPT, Anthropic's Claude, Google's Gemini and xAI's Grok. Meta's Muse Wonder and open-weight models such as Kimi K3.1 Pro are the main alternatives.

### What is better than ChatGPT?

On Arena, Gemini 4 Argon and Claude Opus 5.5 outrank every OpenAI model, and Opus 5.5 does too on Artificial Analysis (59 against 57 for GPT-6 Astra). For price per task, GPT-6.1 Sol is the cheapest model scoring 55 in the value table.
