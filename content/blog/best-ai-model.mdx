As of November 6, 2026, **Claude Opus 5.5** leads the coding boards, **Gemini 4 Argon** led Arena and Mercor's agent board among models then on sale, and **GPT-6.1 Sol** is the value pick for API work. **Gemini 4 Carbon** topped 3 boards on November 6, when only cyber defenders had it; it opened to Google AI Ultra and the API on November 10. On November 9, 2026, Artificial Analysis added Claude Fable 5.2 to the top of its Intelligence Index, so the Intelligence Index scores in the tables below are from November 6.

Rankings like this go stale within weeks, so every table below carries its capture date.

### Preference, Independent Index, Agent Boards

Each board answers a different question, which is why the order changes.

Arena ranks models by blind human preference. It rewards answers people like, not answers that are correct. On the November 6 board, Carbon's 1526 was flagged preliminary and rested on 4,483 votes, against 18,399 for Argon and 14,849 for Opus 5.5.

Artificial Analysis runs every model itself, and the effort setting changes the result a lot. Opus 5.5 scores 59 at max effort and 52 at high. When Google launched Carbon it cited 59 on the index; the November 6 capture shows 60. On November 9, 2026, Artificial Analysis listed Claude Fable 5.2 at 65 at max effort ($26.54 per task) and 62 at high effort ($9.08), ahead of Carbon at 60 and Opus 5.5 at 59 ($10.04), so even one board moves.

Mercor and Terminal-Bench test agents, so each score covers the model plus its tooling. Terminal-Bench lists the agent beside every result: Claude Code for the Claude models and Codex for the OpenAI models. Mercor's live board is newer than its own September 23 "best models" page, which predates the Argon and Carbon launches and still puts Opus 5.5 first.

Vendor tables are self-run. Anthropic's own [Opus 5.5 post](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5) warns that "benchmark margins have become a less reliable guide to real-world differences", and its Terminal-Bench 4.0 row compares Opus 5.5 at xhigh effort with GPT-6 Astra at high effort, as reported by OpenAI. Anthropic says each figure is that model's highest score, but the settings differ.

Benchmarks also expire. OpenAI [stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/) because of flawed tests and contamination, and now recommends SWE-bench Pro. A coding score from an older leaderboard may measure a test at least one lab has stopped reporting.

### What We Tested, and What We Only Cite

Every benchmark score on this page is cited from its owner with a date. We did not rerun any of them. The one dataset we produced ourselves at geotoolbox is a panel: on November 6, 2026 we put 8 model-choice questions to 5 AI engines and logged every source each one cited. The 5 were a logged-out ChatGPT scrape plus an API call, Gemini, Google AI Mode, Perplexity's Sonar API and a Claude agent with web search.

The engines cited 183 distinct domains. Of those, 152 appeared in only one engine, and only 15 were cited by 3 or more, almost all vendor documentation plus leaderboards: anthropic.com, openai.com, x.ai, Artificial Analysis, Arena, BenchLM and LLM Stats. Outside that shared core, the engines mostly disagree on what to cite.

## Best AI Model for Coding

For agentic coding, **Claude Opus 5.5** is the best LLM on the evidence that does not come from a vendor's own launch post, with Claude Sonnet 5.5 second and GPT-6.1 Sol the one to pick when cost matters.

The official Terminal-Bench board re-graded its 4.0 runs as version 4.1 on November 9, 2026, and every score stayed inside its earlier 95% confidence interval. On it, Opus 5.5 scores 65.8% with Claude Code and Sonnet 5.5 63.9%. GPT-6.1 Sol (60.9%) and GPT-6 Astra (60.3%) follow with Codex, within each other's confidence intervals, and Fable 5.1's best run scores 57.6%. Fable 5.2 has no run on it yet. The board also lists what each run cost, and GPT-6.1 Sol's was a fraction of Opus 5.5's and Sonnet 5.5's. For a deeper look at the four Claude tiers, see [Claude models compared](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-models-compared).

Mercor's live [APEX-SWE board](https://www.mercor.com/apex/apex-swe-leaderboard/) agrees on the top 2: Opus 5.5 at 67.6% and Sonnet 5.5 at 66.4%, then Opus 5 at 63.7% and Fable 5.1 at 63.6% among the entries its chart shows. Mercor reports each model with the harness that scores it highest, here Terminus-2.

Open weights are close on this one benchmark. [Moonshot's model card](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro) reports 65.7% for Kimi K3.1 Pro on Terminal-Bench 4.0 using the Claude Code harness, and its table takes other models' scores from their blog posts or the official leaderboard, so it is a vendor figure like any other.

The coding tool wraps the model and changes the score, so it matters too. For deeper dives, see our [Kimi K3 vs Claude comparison](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/kimi-k3-vs-claude), the [GPT-6 Astra breakdown](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/gpt-6-astra) and the [Claude Fable 5.1 review](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-fable-5-1).

## Best AI Model for Writing, Research, and Long Documents

For knowledge work the boards split. Opus 5.5 tops [Artificial Analysis's GDPval-AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gdpval-aa) at 1866, within the margin of Kimi K3.1 Pro (1861) and Muse Wonder Full (1857); Claude Fable 5.2, added to the board on November 9, 2026, scores 1831 at max effort and 1718 at high effort, below Opus 5.5 at max, but at API defaults Fable 5.2 (high, 1718) leads Opus 5.5 (medium, 1593). Gemini 4 Argon sits far down that board at 1633. Argon beats Opus 5.5 on Mercor's professional-tasks board (82.4% to 73.5%) and sits above Opus on Arena. Test both on your own documents.

Context size is the one hard number here, and the top models are close. Claude Opus 5.5, Fable 5.2 and Sonnet 5.5 all take 1M tokens. [GPT-6 Astra](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-astra) has a 1,050,000-token window, of which up to 922,000 can be input and 128,000 output. Gemini 4 Argon offers a 1M unified input-output limit through the API, and Grok 4.7 stops at 500K.

Writing quality has no neutral score beyond preference votes, and Arena's votes put Gemini, Meta and Claude models on top. For research, the major apps each ship a research mode (ChatGPT's [plans page](https://chatgpt.com/pricing) lists deep research on every plan, for example). We found no independent head-to-head test of them, so we do not rank them. For broader comparisons of the apps themselves, see [Claude vs ChatGPT](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-vs-chatgpt), [Claude vs Gemini](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-vs-gemini) and [Gemini vs ChatGPT](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/gemini-vs-chatgpt).

## Best Open-Weight Models

The open-weight leader is **Kimi K3.1 Pro**. Artificial Analysis lists it as the highest-ranked of the 40 open-weights models on its 99-model board, at 46 (rechecked November 8, 2026). That is about 14 points behind Carbon (60) and 13 behind Opus 5.5 (59). The [model card](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro) lists 2.8T total parameters with 104B active, under a custom Kimi K3.1 Pro License.

Behind it, [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source) shows DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (max) at 33 and GLM-5.3 (max) at 32, a wide drop from Kimi. "Open weights" is not "open source": read each license before you build a product on a model. Our guides to the [best open-source LLMs](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/best-open-source-llms) and to [Chinese AI models compared](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/chinese-ai-models-compared) cover licenses and hosting in more detail, and our guide to [running an LLM locally](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/run-llm-locally) covers the hardware side.

## How to Choose for Your Own Work

A leaderboard shortlists models. Your own tasks decide.

1. Check each vendor's data terms first if you handle client or customer data.
2. Shortlist 3 models that rank in the top 5 on at least 2 boards for your kind of work.
3. Match the settings. Compare scores at the same effort level and with the same tool, because both move results.
4. Run 20 to 50 of your own tasks through the tool you will use: the API, Claude Code, Codex, or the chat app.
5. Score whether the answer is correct, what the run cost, and how long it took.
6. Re-test when a vendor ships. Google released Carbon about 2 weeks after Argon, so a shortlist can be out of date within a month.

For a primer on why models behave differently on the same prompt, see [how AI models think](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/how-ai-models-think).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is the best AI model right now?

It depends on the job. Claude Opus 5.5 is the pick for coding, Gemini 4 Argon for Arena and agent scores among models on sale on November 6, and GPT-6.1 Sol for value per task. Gemini 4 Carbon scored higher on 3 boards as of November 6 and opened to Ultra and the API on November 10. Claude Fable 5.2 has led Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index since November 9, 2026, but has no independent coding-board run yet and trails Opus 5.5 on GDPval-AA at max effort.

### Is Gemini 4 Carbon available to the public?

Partly. Since November 10, 2026 it has been rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers and is on sale through the API ($2 / $10 per million tokens until November 30, then $4 / $20). AI Pro and free plans do not include it.

### What are the major AI model families?

The major families are OpenAI's GPT, Anthropic's Claude, Google's Gemini and xAI's Grok. Meta's Muse Wonder and open-weight models such as Kimi K3.1 Pro are the main alternatives.

### What is better than ChatGPT?

On Arena, Gemini 4 Argon and Claude Opus 5.5 outrank every OpenAI model, and Opus 5.5 does too on Artificial Analysis on the November 6 capture (59 against 57 for GPT-6 Astra). For price per task, GPT-6.1 Sol is the cheapest model scoring 55 in the value table.
