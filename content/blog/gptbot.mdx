GPTBot is OpenAI's web crawler that collects public pages to train its models. If you have seen it in your logs and wondered whether to let it in, the honest answer takes one distinction most guides skip.

Blocking GPTBot does not remove you from ChatGPT. Training and search run on different crawlers, so the choice is narrower than "block AI or not." This guide covers what GPTBot actually does, how to allow or block it in one line, and how to make that call without accidentally opting out of the thing you wanted to keep.

## What Is GPTBot?

GPTBot is the crawler OpenAI uses to gather publicly available web content for training its generative models. It reads text to learn language and facts, and per [OpenAI's crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) it identifies itself with a user agent ending in `+https://openai.com/gptbot`.

Two things it is not. It is not a search indexer: unlike Googlebot, GPTBot does not build a ranking index, it feeds model training. And it is not a backdoor: OpenAI states GPTBot does not access pages behind a paywall or login, and does not collect personally identifiable information.

It also respects robots.txt. That single fact is what makes the rest of this article simple: controlling GPTBot is a one-line decision you make at the root of your domain, not a firewall project. The harder part is knowing what that decision actually changes, which is the next section.

## What GPTBot Crawls, and What It Skips

GPTBot crawls publicly reachable pages, the same ones any visitor or search bot can open, and pulls the text into OpenAI's training data.

What it leaves alone matters as much as what it takes. It does not crawl pages disallowed in robots.txt, content behind authentication or a paywall, or anything that needs a form submission. OpenAI also says it screens out personally identifiable information.

The practical read: if a page is public and not disallowed, assume GPTBot can use it for training. If it sits behind a login, it is already out of reach. Everything in between, the public marketing site, the blog, the docs, is what your robots.txt decision governs.

## GPTBot Is Not the Only OpenAI Bot, and That Changes Your Decision

This is the part that trips up almost every "should you block GPTBot" guide. OpenAI runs four crawlers, and they do different jobs.

| Bot | What it does | Block it and you... |
|---|---|---|
| **GPTBot** | Collects content to train OpenAI's models | opt out of model training |
| **OAI-SearchBot** | Builds the index behind ChatGPT search | disappear from ChatGPT search answers |
| **ChatGPT-User** | Fetches a page live when a user asks ChatGPT to read it | block the on-demand reads users asked for |
| **OAI-AdsBot** | Validates ad landing pages | affect ad checks only |

The line that matters: **blocking GPTBot opts you out of training, not out of ChatGPT search.** Visibility in ChatGPT's answers runs through OAI-SearchBot and ChatGPT-User, which are separate tokens. OpenAI is explicit that sites opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not appear in ChatGPT search, and that this is independent from GPTBot.

So the common fear, "if I block GPTBot I vanish from ChatGPT," is backwards. You can block training and stay fully citable in search, or allow training and still be invisible if OAI-SearchBot is blocked. Different decisions, different bots.

## Should You Allow or Block GPTBot?

Start from what blocking GPTBot actually costs and protects, not from a general unease about AI.

| | Allow GPTBot | Block GPTBot |
|---|---|---|
| **Training** | Your public content can train future OpenAI models | Your content is opted out of training |
| **ChatGPT search visibility** | Unaffected (that runs on OAI-SearchBot) | Unaffected (that runs on OAI-SearchBot) |
| **Best fit** | Most sites that want to be part of the AI ecosystem | Publishers protecting proprietary content, or sites under heavy crawl load |
| **Main cost** | Your text feeds models with no click back | Your content is absent from the model's trained knowledge over time |

The myth to drop is that allowing GPTBot "gets you into ChatGPT." Live citations come from OAI-SearchBot and ChatGPT-User. Allowing GPTBot only feeds the training corpus, a slower and fuzzier channel than search, and not the lever for showing up in answers today.

That said, do not overcorrect into "blocking GPTBot does nothing." It has one real effect: your content stops being absorbed into the model's trained knowledge, so it cannot surface from what the model simply knows, only from live search. That channel is harder to measure than a citation, but it is not zero.

So the trade is narrow. Allow GPTBot if you want your content in the training corpus and have the server capacity. Block it if you are protecting content from training, or if the crawl itself is costing you, since aggressive AI crawling that drives up bandwidth and server load has become a common complaint among site owners. Most teams end up selective rather than all-or-nothing: block the bots whose job you object to, keep the ones that put you in answers.

## How to Allow or Block GPTBot in robots.txt

Allowing GPTBot is the default. If you do nothing, it crawls whatever the rest of your robots.txt already permits. To block it from training on your site, disallow it at the root:

```
User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /
```

To opt out of training but stay in ChatGPT search, block only GPTBot and leave OAI-SearchBot and ChatGPT-User alone. That selective setup is what most publishers actually want. To remove yourself from everything OpenAI, block all three:

```
User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /

User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Disallow: /

User-agent: ChatGPT-User
Disallow: /
```

Put the file at the root of your domain, at `yourdomain.com/robots.txt`, and remember each crawler reads only its own block. ## Does Blocking GPTBot Actually Work?

Mostly yes, with two caveats.

GPTBot honors robots.txt, so a correct Disallow stops the real crawler, and that behavior is reliable in practice. But robots.txt is [a published policy, not a wall](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2023/12/no-robotstxt-how-ask-chatgpt-and-google-bard-not-use-your-website-training), and two things sit outside it.

First, your CDN may already be deciding for you. Cloudflare now [blocks AI crawlers by default for new domains](https://www.cloudflare.com/press/press-releases/2025/cloudflare-just-changed-how-ai-crawlers-scrape-the-internet-at-large/), so GPTBot can be stopped at the edge even when your robots.txt says nothing, which surprises owners who never set it. If you want GPTBot in and it is missing, check your CDN's bot rules, not just your robots.txt. In our testing, this is the most common GPTBot surprise: a site that meant to allow the crawler, quietly blocked at the edge by a default it never set.

Second, robots.txt does nothing against impostors. The GPTBot user agent is trivially copied, and [a meaningful share of traffic claiming to be an AI crawler is faked](https://www.humansecurity.com/learn/blog/ai-crawler-spoofing-chatgpt-mistral-perplexity/). A Disallow stops the real, well-behaved GPTBot; it does not stop a scraper wearing its name. For that you need the next step.

## How to Confirm a Request Is Really GPTBot

Because the user agent can be spoofed, do not trust the name alone. OpenAI publishes the IP ranges its crawlers use, so you can verify any request claiming to be GPTBot against the official list at [openai.com/gptbot.json](https://openai.com/gptbot.json). If the requesting IP is not in that range, it is not GPTBot, whatever the user agent says.

The robust check is the standard one for any crawler: confirm the IP appears in the published range, and where supported, that a reverse DNS lookup resolves to the operator and forward-resolves back. Genuine GPTBot traffic passes; a scraper borrowing the name does not. This matters most when you are deciding whether to rate-limit at the server: you want to throttle the impostors, not the real crawler you chose to allow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does blocking GPTBot remove me from ChatGPT?**
No. GPTBot only feeds model training. Your presence in ChatGPT's live search answers runs through a separate crawler, OAI-SearchBot, plus ChatGPT-User for on-demand reads. Block GPTBot and you opt out of training while staying fully eligible for ChatGPT search, as long as you have not also blocked those two.

**How do I block GPTBot from training but stay in ChatGPT search?**
Disallow only GPTBot in robots.txt and leave OAI-SearchBot and ChatGPT-User allowed. That combination opts your content out of OpenAI's training data while keeping you reachable for ChatGPT's search and user-initiated fetches.

**What is the GPTBot user agent?**
GPTBot identifies itself with a user agent ending in `+https://openai.com/gptbot` (currently a `GPTBot/1.x` token on a Mozilla/AppleWebKit string). The version changes, so match on the `GPTBot` token and the openai.com URL rather than the full string, and verify by IP for anything you act on.

**Does GPTBot respect robots.txt?**
Yes. OpenAI states GPTBot follows robots.txt, and a correct Disallow stops it. Two things it cannot fix: a CDN that blocks GPTBot before robots.txt is read, and scrapers that fake the GPTBot name and ignore your rules entirely.

**Will blocking GPTBot hurt my Google SEO?**
No. GPTBot is OpenAI's crawler and has nothing to do with Googlebot or Google rankings. Blocking GPTBot only affects whether OpenAI can use your content for training; your Google Search visibility is governed by Google's own crawlers.

**How do I know a request is really GPTBot and not a fake?**
Check the source IP against OpenAI's published ranges at openai.com/gptbot.json. The user agent alone proves nothing, since it can be copied, but a request from an IP in OpenAI's official range is genuine. Anything claiming to be GPTBot from outside that range is an impostor you can rate-limit or block.
