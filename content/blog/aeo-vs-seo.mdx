AEO vs SEO is often framed as a contest. Google's documentation says it is not one, at least where Google is concerned: optimizing for AI Overviews and AI Mode is, in its words, "still SEO." Outside Google the picture gets messier, because ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Copilot pick their sources by different rules.

So the useful answer is narrower than the debate. For Google's features, AEO shares most of its work with SEO and skips several tactics sold under its name. Across the other engines, it adds a few things SEO never measured.

## AEO vs SEO at a Glance

**SEO earns a ranked link that someone clicks. AEO earns a place inside an answer that someone reads.** Answer engine optimization (AEO) is the work of getting your content selected, quoted, or cited by answer engines and AI assistants: Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Copilot, and Gemini. Traditional SEO targets the ranked search results. For the full definition, see [what answer engine optimization is](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/what-is-answer-engine-optimization); the [GEO vs AEO vs SEO breakdown](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/geo-vs-aeo-vs-seo) sorts out the GEO label.

## What Stays the Same between AEO and SEO

**For Google's own AI features, the work is the same work.** Google's [guide to optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says it in one sentence: "optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO." Google [announced the guide on May 15, 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/05/a-new-resource-for-optimizing), and the page was last updated on July 10.

The reasoning is mechanical. Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode are "rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems." They use retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), where the model pulls pages from the Search index and writes from what comes back, and query fan-out, where it runs several related searches at once and gathers pages for each.

The eligibility rules therefore barely move:

- A page has to be indexed and eligible to show in Google Search with a snippet
- Its content has to be crawlable, and JavaScript has to render without being blocked
- The site must not have excluded itself from Search generative AI features in Search Console

The best practices carry over too: page experience, duplicate-content cleanup, and useful images and video, plus unique, non-commodity content, meaning first-hand expertise rather than a recap of what is already online, which Google says will likely matter most over the long run.

Authority and freshness carry over as well. A [Microsoft Advertising post on AI search](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/october-2025/optimizing-your-content-for-inclusion-in-ai-search-answers) says crawlability, metadata, internal linking, and backlinks "remain essential," and asks for content that is "fresh, authoritative, structured, and semantically clear." A page with crawl or indexing problems cannot be cited by Google's features, whatever AEO work you add. Our [AI SEO guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/ai-seo) covers that foundation, and [getting cited in AI Overviews](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/google-ai-overviews-seo) covers the Google-specific mechanics.

Google's answer is "not required for our features," not "wrong." Microsoft goes further for its own assistant: the same Microsoft Advertising post recommends Q&A formats, lists and tables, and schema, and says assistants like Copilot parse pages into smaller pieces. Each is a company describing its own system, so neither settles what ChatGPT or Perplexity do, and that gap is where the real differences sit.

## Where AEO Differs from SEO

The picture shifts once you look past Google's own features: the engines pick sources differently, sources beyond your own site show up in answers, and the competition is per sub-question instead of per keyword.

### The Engines Do Not Agree with Google

An [Ahrefs study of 15,000 long-tail prompts](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-overlap/) found that only 12% of the links cited by AI assistants also ranked in Google's top 10 for the same prompt, as an unweighted average across the assistants and citation types it tested. Perplexity was the outlier at 28.6%, the rest hovered around 8%, and by Ahrefs's count about 80% of the citations fell outside Google's top 100 for the query. Ahrefs notes that Perplexity runs its own index rather than Google's or Bing's, and it aligned most closely of the assistants.

Google's AI Overviews sit closer to the results page, but the studies disagree on how close.

Some of the spread is definitions. [seoClarity](https://www.seoclarity.net/research/aio-rankings-overlap) asks whether an answer contains even one ranking page, a generous test. Ahrefs asks what share of all cited URLs rank. BrightEdge [counts citations that rank organically at all](https://www.brightedge.com/resources/weekly-ai-search-insights/rank-overlap-after-16-months-of-aio), and its page also says only 16.7% of citations come from top-10 results without stating the base. The rest is method: [Ahrefs's earlier study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-rankings-ai-citations/) counted only the top three most visible citations per answer, its 2026 study analyzed 4 million cited URLs, and it also [improved how it detects citations](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/) in between. Ahrefs still treats the 76%-to-38% drop as real and points to Gemini 3, which it says began powering AI Overviews in January 2026. Quote any of these without its definition and you are quoting noise. All four studies come from SEO software vendors, so weigh them as you would weigh ours.

What holds across the studies: ranking well gets a page into contention for Google's features, and it guarantees nothing. On ChatGPT, Gemini, and Copilot, most cited pages do not rank for the prompt in Google at all, according to Ahrefs's 2025 data. Our [GEO vs SEO budget guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/geo-vs-seo) works through what that means for spend.

### Sources Beyond Your Site Get Cited

Among AI Overview citations that did not rank in Google's top 100 for the query, [Ahrefs found](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/) that 18.2% were YouTube URLs, and those non-ranking YouTube links alone made up 5.6% of every URL cited in its dataset. Google's guide points at the same mechanism: its generative features can show what people say about products and services "across the web, including in blogs, videos, and forum discussions."

For most teams that makes this an operations problem before a content problem: someone has to own places you do not control, such as review sites, forums, video, and how other publications describe you. Google's warning about inauthentic mentions still applies. The aim is accuracy where you are already discussed. Our guide to [entity SEO and the knowledge graph](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/entity-seo) covers how engines tie those mentions back to your brand.

### The Competition Is per Sub-Question

Google's guide gives its own [fan-out](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/query-fan-out) example. A question about how to fix a lawn full of weeds can spawn searches for "best herbicides for lawns," "remove weeds without chemicals," and "how to prevent weeds in lawn." A page can win one of those sub-searches without ranking for the original query. Ahrefs suggests its 76%-to-38% drop may reflect the same thing: AI Overviews leaning more on the results for fan-out queries than on the results for the typed query.

Google warns against mass-producing pages for query variations to manipulate AI responses. The better response is one page that answers the follow-ups a reader would ask next, with separate pages only where readers need them.

## The New Work AEO Adds

**Strip out what SEO already covers and what Google says to skip, and a short list of tasks remains.** This is our list, built from the evidence above, not a tested playbook.

1. **Build a prompt set.** Write the questions your buyers would ask an AI, phrased the way they would type them, grouped by intent. It replaces the keyword list as the thing you track.
2. **Check access.** Engines fetch with their own crawlers, and Perplexity runs its own index, so being in Google's index does not settle the rest.
3. **Own the third-party layer.** Someone has to keep the descriptions on review sites, forums, and video accurate. That is PR, community, or partnerships work rather than SEO work.
4. **Sample and report.** Run the prompt set on a schedule, record which sources get cited, and report rates with the number of runs behind them.

The prompt set and the sampling are measurement work, and the access check is technical. So do you need a separate AEO team? No. You need someone to own each task, and none of them has to be a new hire.

## How Measurement Changes

Google's reports cover Google's features, and Microsoft Clarity's Citations report covers some AI experiences it does not name. Anything beyond those needs a manual sample or a tracker. Rank tracking has no direct equivalent for AI-generated answers, so AEO reporting combines first-party reports with a sample. Disclosure: geotoolbox sells a paid AI visibility tracker.

Start with the free sources. The [Search Console help page](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) says the generative AI performance report reached all websites worldwide as of August 31, 2026. It covers AI Overviews and AI Mode and shows impressions by page, country, device, and date. Microsoft [announced Clarity Citations as generally available](https://clarity.microsoft.com/blog/citations-now-generally-available/) on May 13, 2026, with reports on page citations, grounding queries, share of authority, and AI referral traffic across "supported AI experiences." It does not name them, so check its documentation before assuming Copilot coverage.

In GA4, the "AI Assistant" channel groups visits from sources like ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Copilot, and Grok. Per [Google's channel definitions](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9756891), it excludes AI Overviews and AI Mode. Perplexity is not among the examples Google names, so check your referral report for it. Google assigns the channel from the referrer or a utm_medium of ai-assistant, so a visit with neither does not land in it.

Google's reports do not show what ChatGPT or Perplexity say about you, and Clarity does not say which experiences it covers. Beyond them you need a manual sample or a tracker, and Google's guide cautions against third-party tools that "promise ranking success or claim to use 'internal' Google metrics," because none has access to Google's internal ranking or AI systems. Trust a tool only for what it can observe: the answers it fetched.

Then there is variance. A single run of a prompt proves little, because AI answers are probabilistic, [as Ahrefs notes](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/), so track a fixed set of buyer questions on a schedule and report rates, not screenshots.

Say you track 15 prompts on 3 engines and repeat the set weekly. Over a month that is roughly 180 answers, and "cited in 27 of 60 ChatGPT answers" is a number you can defend where "ChatGPT mentioned us" is not.

## Which One to Prioritize

**Do SEO first, then add AEO as a measurement and distribution layer sized to how much of your audience actually asks AI.** That is our judgment, and a workable AEO strategy is smaller than many checklists suggest.

The one exception to "SEO first" is access. If an AI crawler cannot fetch your pages, whether from a robots.txt rule, a bot-management block, or JavaScript-only rendering, most of your own content work cannot reach that engine.

When is AEO not worth extra effort yet? When analytics show few AI-assistant sessions and your buyers do not research with chatbots. The GA4 channel misses visits without a referrer, so check before you conclude. ## How to Judge AEO Advice

Treat AEO advice as claimed until someone shows it was tested. Three questions sort most of it.

Who is the primary source? For how Google's features work, that is Google's documentation. For Copilot it is Microsoft's. Each describes its own system. For ChatGPT and Perplexity we found little primary guidance on source selection, only third-party measurements.

What exactly was measured? An overlap figure means little until you know whether it counts URLs or answers, the top 10 or the top 20, and which engine and month. The overlap table above shows one topic producing very different numbers.

Does the claim survive without the seller? A recommendation that only makes sense if you buy a tool, a file generator, or a retainer deserves a second source. We have not run our own citation study here, so every figure is someone else's.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is AEO just SEO with a new name?

On SEO vs AEO, Google says yes for its own AI features: optimizing for generative AI search "is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO." Outside Google it is SEO plus an added layer: other engines pick sources that often differ from Google's rankings, and success is measured by sampling answers.

### Will AEO replace SEO, or is SEO dead?

No. Google's AI features run on its core Search ranking systems, and [seoClarity found](https://www.seoclarity.net/research/aio-rankings-overlap) that 94% of the AI Overviews it analyzed cited at least one URL from the top 20 organic results. Our piece on [whether SEO is dead](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/is-seo-dead) goes through what the data shows about clicks.

### Is AEO the same as GEO?

The labels overlap. Google's guide calls both "terms you may see used to describe work specifically focused on improving visibility in AI search experiences." Some guides also fold featured snippets and voice answers into AEO, so check which meaning a page intends.

### If I rank first, will AI cite me?

Not reliably. For Google AI Overviews, [seoClarity's data](https://www.seoclarity.net/research/aio-rankings-overlap) shows position-one URLs cited 43% of the time, falling to 7% at position 20. For other assistants, Ahrefs's August 11, 2025 study of 15,000 prompts found that only 12% of cited URLs ranked in Google's top 10 on average, so most citations come from pages outside Google's top 10.
