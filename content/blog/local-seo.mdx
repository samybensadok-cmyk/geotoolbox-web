Local SEO is how a business gets found by people nearby: in the Google map pack, in the local results under it, and now in the answers ChatGPT and Google AI Mode give when someone asks for the best plumber in town.

At geotoolbox we asked ChatGPT and Google AI Mode for the best plumbers, dentists, roofers and personal injury lawyers in three US cities, 48 answers in all. ChatGPT and AI Mode showed very different sources.

In our test, every AI Mode answer showed Google's own business listings. ChatGPT leaned on best-of lists and industry directories, and linked Yelp pages in a quarter of its answers. Most of the businesses either engine named were missing from the Google Maps top 20 for a similar search.

## What Is Local SEO?

**Local SEO** is the work of getting a business shown when people nearby search for what it sells. It covers the Google Business Profile, the website, reviews and the listings other sites keep about the business.

Local searches are answered in three places now: the map pack, the local organic results and AI answers.

The **map pack**, also called the local pack, is the box of businesses shown with a map in Google Search; Google Maps itself is a related surface. Both run on Google Business Profile data: categories, reviews, hours and the business's location relative to the searcher.

The **local organic results** under the map pack are ordinary web rankings. Your service pages, location pages and links compete there, so the [technical basics](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/technical-seo) still apply.

The third place is the **AI answer**: ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Mode and the AI Overviews at the top of some Google results. Ask ChatGPT or Google AI Mode for the best roofer in Denver and you get a short list of named companies, backed by different sources again. We tested ChatGPT and AI Mode; Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot may lean on other sources.

Local SEO applies to storefronts and to service-area businesses. A storefront serves customers at its address: a dental office, a restaurant, a shop. A **service-area business** travels to customers, like a plumber or a roofer.

Your website backs up the profile: one page per core service, pages for the areas served when they have real local content, and the same business name, address and phone number as the profile.

## How Google Ranks Local Businesses

Google's own help page on [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity," and describes the third factor, under the heading prominence, as "how well-known a business is."

**Relevance** is how well a profile matches the search. Google [asks for](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) "complete and detailed business info" to judge it, and lists your address, hours, category and attributes such as parking.

**Distance** is how far the business is from the customer who is searching. If a searcher doesn't share a location, Google [uses what it knows](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) about where they are. You can't change your distance from a given searcher, which is why a business can rank first on one street and much lower a few miles away.

**Prominence** is reputation. Google's [ranking page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) counts how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have, states that "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking," and says there's no way to pay for a better local position.

Google doesn't publish weights. The closest thing is the [Whitespark Local Search Ranking Factors survey](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/), whose 2026 edition, published November 6, 2025, has local SEO practitioners score 187 factors. Whitespark sells citation and local SEO services, and the survey records practitioners' informed opinion rather than measurement.

Its seven highest-scored map pack factors:

1. Primary Business Profile category
2. Distance between searcher and business
3. Keywords in the business name
4. A physical address in the city being searched
5. Being open at the time of the search
6. High Google ratings
7. An address that shows on the profile instead of being hidden

Number 3 is a trap. Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say "unnecessary information" in a business name isn't permitted and can get a profile suspended.

For 2026, the [survey's author](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) reports review and behavioral signals gaining importance, while citations lost about half a percentage point, continuing a decline from earlier editions.

The 2026 edition also [scored AI search visibility](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) separately for the first time. Its top AI factor is a business's presence on expert-curated "best of" lists, and the report notes that 3 of the top 5 AI factors are citation factors.

## How AI Assistants Pick Local Businesses

AI tools are already a local discovery channel. In the [Local Consumer Review Survey 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) of 1,002 US adults by BrightLocal, which sells local SEO software, use of ChatGPT and other AI tools for local recommendations rose from 6% to 45% in a year.

To see where those recommendations come from, we ran a test on November 13, 2026. We asked "What are the best [service] in [city]?" for plumbers, dentists, roofers and personal injury lawyers in Austin, Columbus and Denver: 12 prompts, each run twice in ChatGPT and twice in Google AI Mode, 48 answers in all.

ChatGPT was queried logged out from the US with web search forced on, and the city was in the prompt. AI Mode's location was set to the city itself.

For a baseline, we pulled the Google Maps top 20 for "[service] in [city]" in each city, ads excluded. We counted every source each answer showed, matched business names to Maps automatically and checked borderline matches by hand.

In numbers: 109 of AI Mode's 113 references pointed to Google place listings, the Business Profile data behind each card: name, rating, review count, address. The other 4 were Thumbtack (3) and a lawyer directory (1), and 135 of the 137 cards also linked the business's website.

ChatGPT showed 130 sources across its 24 answers: 66 best-of lists (Expertise.com, city ranking sites), 30 industry directories (Avvo for lawyers, Healthgrades and WebMD for dentists), 14 review sites, 10 roofer websites (one a roofer's own best-of post), 9 state licensing or bar pages and 1 Google Maps page. Prompt design may explain part of the gap, so read our split as what "best [service] in [city]" questions return.

### Where ChatGPT's Business Cards Get Their Numbers

ChatGPT usually shows business cards with a rating and review count. For 23 of the 32 businesses we could match to Google Maps, the card showed fewer than half of Google's reviews, while 8 matched Google's count within 2%.

In 6 of the 24 answers, ChatGPT linked businesses or their ratings to Yelp pages tagged as a Yelp data feed (`utm_campaign=yelp_feed`). That fits Axios's [July 2026 report](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/23/yelp-reviews-chatgpt-geo-partnership) that Yelp is licensing reviews, ratings, photos and business details to OpenAI for ChatGPT answers. So the card next to your name may carry your Yelp numbers rather than your Google ones.

For more on how the retrieval step works, see [how ChatGPT search works](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/how-chatgpt-search-works) and our guide to [Google AI Mode SEO](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/google-ai-mode-seo).

## Set Up Your Google Business Profile to Rank

The profile feeds the map pack and shows up in AI Mode's answers, so it comes first. Most of the work is getting a few fields exactly right.

### Primary Category

Pick the primary category that names your core business ("Plumber", not "Water heater installation service"), then add secondary categories only for services you offer. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) ask you to "choose the fewest number of categories it takes to describe your overall core business."

### Business Name

Use your real-world name, as it appears on your storefront, website and invoices, with no city or service added.

### Address or Service Area

A storefront shows its address. A service-area business hides it and lists the areas it serves; Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say that area shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time from your base, though some businesses can justify more. A hybrid, like an auto shop that also does roadside calls, can show its address and list a service area.

Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) rule out three kinds of address for a profile:

- a virtual office
- a P.O. box
- a mailbox at a remote location

If you don't operate from an address, don't put a profile there.

### Hours, Services and Attributes

Fill in every service with a short description, set holiday hours before holidays, and add the attributes that apply to you (wheelchair access, online appointments, languages spoken). Keep hours accurate, and never claim hours you don't keep.

### Photos and Posts

Upload real photos of your work, team, vehicles and premises, and keep adding them. Use profile posts for offers, events and news. Google's [ranking page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) doesn't mention posts, so write them for customers.

### Multiple Locations

Give each branch its own profile and its own page on your site, with that branch's hours, services and staff. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say not to "create more than one page for each location of your business," so merge or remove any duplicate profile for the same branch.

Individual practitioners, such as a dentist in a group practice, have separate rules and may get their own profile.

### Verification and Suspensions

Verification problems and suspensions came up again and again in the 40 Reddit threads and 4 Google Business Profile Help Community threads we read for this guide. The same triggers repeat, and Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) rule out most of them:

- a keyword-stuffed business name
- a virtual office, or a coworking desk without signage and staff, as the address
- a service-area business showing a home address
- several profiles for one location

Keep documents that tie the business to the address (utility bills, a business license, photos of signage), and be ready for Google to ask for video verification. A suspended profile can be reinstated through Google's appeal process, so have that evidence ready before you file.

## Get More Reviews Without Breaking the Rules

Reviews are the part of prominence you can grow every week.

Customers check how fresh reviews are too. In the [BrightLocal survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), 74% of consumers look for reviews written in the last three months, and 32% look for reviews from the last two weeks. A profile with hundreds of reviews and nothing new since spring looks stale.

### How to Get More Google Reviews

What works is plain: ask every customer, at the moment the job is done, with a direct link to your review form. Put the link in the invoice email, on the receipt and in a follow-up text. Staff can ask in person, without pressure: Google's [policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) says businesses shouldn't require or pressure customers to review while on the premises.

### Replying to and Reporting Reviews

Reply to reviews, especially the negative ones: a short, specific reply shows the next reader that someone runs the business.

For fake or abusive reviews, flag them from your profile. Google's [review reporting page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en) says you can report any review, but only those that break its policies are removed; evaluation typically takes several days, and a rejected report gets one appeal. Google has a separate route for extortion scams that threaten negative reviews.

### What Google and the FTC Don't Allow

Two sets of rules apply in the US. Google's [Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) bans incentives "such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services" in exchange for a review, and bans selectively soliciting positive reviews, often called review gating. The FTC's [rule on fake reviews](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials), in effect since October 21, 2024, adds civil penalties for knowing violations.

## Citations and the Lists AI Answers Read

A **local citation** is any mention of your business name, address and phone number (NAP) on another site: directories, data aggregators, review platforms, your chamber of commerce, industry associations.

Citations used to be a core map pack lever. NAP consistency still matters for matching: Google, and any AI tool that pulls from these sites, has to tell that "Smith Plumbing LLC, Suite 4" and "Smith Plumbing" are the same company. 

Citation tools do this at scale: Whitespark's Local Citation Finder tracks your listings and finds your competitors' citations, and BrightLocal's Citation Builder builds and cleans up listings. Fix the wrong ones and stop there; chasing a hundred low-value directories is unlikely to move rankings. A service-area business that hides a home address shouldn't publish it in listings or markup either.

In the US, start with the listings people and platforms use most:

- **Apple Business** (formerly Apple Business Connect), where you manage your Apple Maps listing
- **Bing Places for Business**, for Bing Maps and Bing local results
- **Yelp**, which ChatGPT linked often in our test
- **Facebook** and the **Better Business Bureau**
- Your other **social profiles** (Instagram, LinkedIn), with the same name, address and phone; the 2026 [Whitespark survey](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) added social signals back as a factor group and lists social profiles linked to the Business Profile among its factors
- The main directory for your trade: Avvo or Justia for lawyers, Healthgrades or Zocdoc for dentists and doctors, Angi or Houzz for home services

Then look at the lists ChatGPT showed in our test. Across its 24 answers, the most frequent sources were Avvo (18) and Expertise.com (14), then Yelp (8) and ranking sites for dentists and roofers. Some of these lists build their rankings from Google reviews ([FlexiLocal](https://reviews.flexilocal.com/l/us/texas/austin/plumbers), for one, sorts by rating, then number of reviews), so the review work above feeds them too.

To find yours, ask ChatGPT for the best businesses in your category and city, and open the sources it shows. Some lists are editorial and accept nominations or run review processes. Others are pay-to-play. Check how a list chooses businesses before paying for a spot, and don't buy a badge from a site no AI answer cites.

For more on what makes AI tools cite a page, see our breakdown of [ChatGPT citations](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/chatgpt-citations).

## Location Pages, Schema and Local Links

Your website does double duty in local SEO. It ranks on its own in the local organic results, and it confirms what your profile says.

### Service Pages and Local Keywords

Start with the local keywords customers type. Google's [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) tells site owners to "think about the words that a user might search for." Local searches either include a place ("dentist in Columbus") or leave it out ("emergency dentist"). Map each service and area to one page instead of asking the homepage to rank for everything.

Build one page per core service before you build any city pages. "Water heater repair" deserves its own page with prices or price ranges, what the job involves, photos and reviews that mention it.

Give it a title that names the service and the area, such as "Water Heater Repair in Columbus, OH": Google's [starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says a good title is unique to the page, and the title shapes the link people see in results. Then link each service page to the matching city pages and back, so visitors and crawlers can move between them.

### City Pages Without Doorway Spam

Near-identical city pages built to catch searches and funnel visitors to one place risk being treated as doorways: Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) list doorway abuse as spam, including pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page. A city page earns its place when it has content only that city could have:

- jobs you did there, with photos
- the team or crew that covers it
- local permits, codes or conditions
- reviews from customers in that area

If you can't write that for a town, leave it out and list it in a service-area section instead.

A blog does the same job one level up. Posts that answer questions specific to your area, like frozen pipes in a cold-winter city or roof checks after hail season, give Google and AI tools local material that a national site doesn't have.

### Mobile Pages and Maps

Check every page on a phone. Google indexes the [mobile version](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing) of a site's content, and a visitor standing in a parking lot needs the call button, address and hours without hunting for them. Keep pages fast too; our [technical SEO guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/technical-seo) covers page speed and Core Web Vitals.

An embedded Google map on your contact or location pages helps with directions, and Google's [Maps Embed API](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/embed/get-started) adds one with a simple HTTP request. Google's local ranking page doesn't mention map embeds, so treat the map as a convenience for visitors, not a ranking lever.

### LocalBusiness Schema

Google's [Local Business structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) lets you state your hours, departments, address and other details in a format machines read without guessing. Use the most specific type that fits (Plumber, Dentist, Attorney) and fill in name, address, phone, hours and URL; most SEO plugins can output it.

Google also [says](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) it "does not guarantee that features that consume structured data will show up in search results." Local business schema is data hygiene: it makes your NAP, hours and service area unambiguous for Google and other search engines, and it should match your profile exactly. Our guide to [schema markup for AI](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/schema-markup-for-ai) covers the markup itself.

### Local Links

Local backlinks help twice: in the map pack through prominence, and for your pages in the local organic search results. The useful ones are earned:

- a sponsorship page from a youth team
- a chamber of commerce membership
- a supplier's "where to buy" page
- a story in the local paper about a job or a hire

Local press can also reach AI answers directly. When we asked for the best dentists in Denver, ChatGPT cited the city magazine 5280's top dentists list.

## How to Measure Local SEO

A single "rank" for a local keyword doesn't exist, because of the distance factor above: a report that says "#3 for plumber" describes one point on the map.

### Geo-Grid Rank Tracking

A geo-grid tool checks your map pack position from a set of points around your business (a 7x7 or 9x9 grid, for example) and shows where you rank first, where you drop out, and which competitor takes your place. Several local SEO tools sell it, including BrightLocal's Local Search Grid and Whitespark's Local Ranking Grids.

It is also a quick way to read a drop in rankings. If you fell everywhere, check the profile first: a category change, an edit under review, a suspension. If you fell only at the edges, look at competitors nearer those areas.

### Local Competitor Checks

Look at the businesses that beat you in the grid and in AI answers: their primary category, how many reviews they have and how fast new ones arrive, and which lists and directories name them. Those are the gaps you can close first.

### Business Profile and Analytics

Google's [performance report](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en) lists the searches people used, views, calls, direction requests, website clicks, messages and bookings. Google notes the totals include Google Ads as well as organic results, so don't read them as organic-only if you run ads.

Tag the website link on your profile with UTM parameters (for example `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`), so GA4 groups the traffic that came through that link. ChatGPT's business cards can reuse that link: 52 of the 122 card links in our test carried owners' profile tags, so some ChatGPT clicks will land under your profile campaign.

Search Console covers the local organic side: queries with city names, and the pages that rank for them.

### AI Answer Checks

Check AI answers the way we did: a fixed list of prompts ("best [service] in [city]", "[service] near [neighborhood]"), the same engines each time, several runs per prompt, and a log of the date, the businesses named and the sources shown. Our reruns showed why one check isn't enough.

## Local SEO Checklist

Work through these in order, or use them as a quick local SEO audit of an existing business.

1. **Claim and verify your Google Business Profile**, with your real business name and no added keywords.
2. **Set the primary category** to your core business, and add only the secondary categories that match services you offer.
3. **Fill every field:** services with descriptions, hours (including holidays), attributes, service areas and real photos.
4. **Set up a review routine:** a direct review link sent after every job, no incentives, and a reply to every review.
5. **Make your name, address and phone (NAP) match** on your website, Apple Business, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook and your trade's main directory.
6. **Find the best-of lists and directories** that AI answers show for your category and city, and get onto the legitimate ones.
7. **Build one page per core service**, then city pages only where you have local work to show.
8. **Add LocalBusiness schema** that matches your profile exactly.
9. **Earn a few local backlinks** from sponsorships, associations, suppliers and local press.
10. **Measure monthly:** geo-grid positions, profile calls and clicks, UTM-tagged visits, and a fixed set of AI prompts run more than once.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does local SEO cost?

Doing it yourself costs time and a few tools: the Business Profile and most listings are free, and a geo-grid tracker is a monthly subscription. Agency prices depend on how many locations you have and how competitive your trade is in your city. Ask what the fee covers: profile management, reviews, listings, pages, links and reporting are separate pieces of work.

### Can I do local SEO myself?

Yes. Most of the work that moves results is in your hands: the Business Profile, a review routine, consistent listings and real service pages. Owners have an edge here: they have the photos, job details and customer contacts. Bring in help for technical site problems, for many locations, or when a suspended profile needs an appeal.

### How long does local SEO take?

There's no fixed timeline. Google's [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says some changes "might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months," and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging a change.

### Does local SEO work without a storefront?

Yes. Businesses that visit customers can list service areas instead of an address, per Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en). Hiding the address has a cost: the [Whitespark survey](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) scores a visible address among its top map pack factors. Your service and city pages do the work farther out.
