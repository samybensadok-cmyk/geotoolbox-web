Sonnet vs Opus is the common question, but Claude's current lineup has more tiers than that. As of November 6, 2026, the lineup is **Fable 5.1**, **Opus 5.5**, **Sonnet 5.5** and **Haiku 5.5**, and each has a 1M-token context window on the API.

Anthropic's own guide says to start most workloads on Opus 5.5, while its Claude Code help page calls Sonnet "the right choice for the large majority of coding work." Our reading of that tension: Sonnet 5.5 for well-specified work you can check yourself, at medium effort, Opus 5.5 when the goal is vague or the work runs for hours, Haiku 5.5 for volume and subagents, and Fable (Fable 5.2 since November 9, 2026) only when Opus still falls short at xhigh or max effort. Try a different effort level before you change the tier.

**Update, November 9, 2026:** Anthropic released [Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) on November 9, 2026, with Fable 5.2 at $8 / $40 per MTok. The tables, charts, tests and verdicts below describe the lineup before that release, with Fable 5.1 as the top tier, and we are updating this comparison for Fable 5.2.

Haiku 5.5 is the only tier priced by prompt length: Anthropic's [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) charges the higher rate on any request whose prompt is over 100,000 tokens, and the count includes cache reads and writes. ## Sonnet vs Opus: What Separates Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5

Opus 5.5 costs exactly twice as much per token as Sonnet 5.5 ($4 / $20 against $2 / $10), and on most coding benchmarks the two sit within a few points of each other. Anthropic's own [Sonnet 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5) says the difference shows up elsewhere: "Opus 5.5 remains clearly stronger at complex, open-ended work requiring sustained judgment."

Read the first two rows together. Anthropic's table has Sonnet ahead on Terminal-Bench and the [public leaderboard](https://www.tbench.ai/) has Opus ahead, and the error margins (a ±2.6 point standard error for Opus, 95% confidence intervals of about ±3 points on the board) mean neither gap clearly separates them. Treat the pair as roughly tied. The board itself moved to Terminal-Bench 4.1 on November 9, 2026: it re-graded the saved 4.0 runs with hardened task checkers, and every score stayed within its earlier 95% confidence interval, while Anthropic's launch tables still report 4.0.

Cost is the more useful signal. The leaderboard's job pages for [the Sonnet run](https://hub.harborframework.com/jobs/0e520a82-ad44-56be-b1bd-ff351c5bd923) and [the Opus run](https://hub.harborframework.com/jobs/96ad98a5-70e6-5e7b-bd1e-67eecfbc644c) show that both started on September 29, before Anthropic [halved Sonnet 5.5's cache-read price](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5) on October 7, and that each ran at a 97% cache-hit rate.

At that date cache reads cost $0.20 per MTok on both models, so Sonnet was not half price on most of its tokens. It also used more than twice as many (19.4B tokens against 8.0B), which contributed to a bill about 55% higher than Opus's at September 29 prices.

Three tests published on September 28 and 29, right after the Sonnet 5.5 launch, add hands-on evidence, with one caveat: they predate the October 7 Sonnet cache-read cut, which Anthropic says makes Sonnet around 20% cheaper on most agentic work.

Nate Herk ran seven workflows at high effort and found Opus cost "about $16 more" in total and ran about 46 minutes longer, which he notes is not double the cost. In [his video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7eo-11K2e3c) he reads Anthropic's guidance this way: "if you have a task with an objective definition of done, then try that out with Sonnet 5.5 first."

The other builds show how much the bill depends on the project. In [Leon van Zyl's mini-golf test](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NzyP4VjkrvM), run at extra-high (xhigh) effort, Sonnet 5.5 cost about $26 against about $27 for Opus 5.5, and he sided with Opus: "I would much rather prefer Opus 5.5." In [Brock Mesarich's voxel-world build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn08Kdp998Y), with every model at high effort, Sonnet cost $9.02 against $11.95, and Brock called Sonnet's world "the best overall one." The creators split, and each ran a single prompt, so read them as examples, not rules.

In Claude Code, the `opusplan` alias plans on Opus and executes on Sonnet, per the [model docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config). For what each model does to your bill, see our guide to [reducing Claude Code token costs](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/reduce-claude-code-token-costs).

**Verdict:** Sonnet 5.5 is the cost-saving candidate for well-specified work you can check, started at medium effort, where Anthropic says it costs less per task. At higher effort its edge fades ("At higher settings, it can perform comparably at a similar cost"), and for ambiguous or long-running work the evidence backs Opus 5.5. Compare completed-task cost and quality on your own workload before you standardize on either.

## Haiku 5.5 vs Sonnet 5.5: When the Small Model Is Enough

Haiku 5.5 is cheap enough to change how you build, but it is not a drop-in replacement for Sonnet. Anthropic positions it for quick and repetitive workloads such as summaries, compactions, database queries and classification, and says it pairs well with Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 as a subagent, in the [Haiku 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5).

The benchmark gap explains why. In that post's table, Haiku 5.5 scores 39.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 against 70.6% for Sonnet 5.5, and 1620 against 1840 on GDPval-AA v2.1 (the Sonnet post lists Sonnet at 1844). On FrontierCode 1.1 (Main) the gap is smaller: 46.4% against Sonnet's 52.1% at xhigh effort.

Anthropic's choosing-a-model guide even offers an efficiency-first path that starts a project on Haiku 5.5 and upgrades only where it falls short, and price is why.

At $0.10 / $0.50 per MTok for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, Haiku costs one-twentieth of Sonnet 5.5's input and output rates, and Anthropic says it costs around 75% less to run than Haiku 4.5 on average (list prices for prompts up to 100K tokens fell 90%).

That changes the economics of subagents: a fleet of workers can read files, search and summarize while a bigger model makes the decisions.

Two limits matter. The price steps up to $0.50 / $2.50 once a prompt passes 100,000 tokens, which sharply cuts Haiku's savings on large prompts even though it stays cheaper per token than Sonnet.

And it is a weaker checker: a [Reddit user who reran a set of project tasks as subagents](https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeCode/comments/1x1e7ts/i_tested_haiku_55_sonnet_55_and_opus_55_as_subagents_on_6_real_tasks/) found Haiku close to Opus on code search (44 of 48 files against 45, at $0.44 against $6.17) but marking 30 correct sentences as errors while fact-checking two blog posts, against 0 for Opus.

It is one person's test at high effort, but it fits the pattern: let the small model fetch and sort, and let a stronger one verify.

**Verdict:** use Haiku 5.5 for classification, extraction, routing, search and subagent legwork under 100K tokens per request, and keep Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5 on consequential judgments and final verification.

## Fable 5.1 vs Opus 5.5: Is the Top Tier Worth the Premium?

Fable 5.1 costs $10 / $50 per MTok, which is 2.5 times Opus 5.5 and five times Sonnet 5.5, and Anthropic's own guidance is not to start there.

Its [choosing-a-model guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/choosing-a-model) says most workloads start with Opus 5.5, and that if your evals "at xhigh or max effort still fall short on demanding reasoning or long-horizon agentic work," you move to the top Fable model, which was Fable 5.1 until November 9, 2026 and has been Fable 5.2 since.

That is why Opus can feel expensive but is not the costly tier: Opus 5.5 costs $4 / $20 per MTok, less than Opus 5 did, and Fable is the costly tier: $10 / $50 for Fable 5.1, and $8 / $40 for Fable 5.2 since November 9, 2026.

The benchmark table in the [Opus 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) shows why, and our own [Fable 5.1 guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-fable-5-1) covers the model in depth. Opus 5.5 leads Fable 5.1 on every row Anthropic published for the pair.

Five rows are shown below; the rest range from under a point (Chartography, 89.0% against 88.4%) to six points (Terminal-Bench-Science, 58.7% against 52.6%). Anthropic adds that benchmark margins have become a less reliable guide at this capability level and that, in its own use, the gap between the two is narrower than these scores suggest.

On the Artificial Analysis board below, Fable 5.1 also scores under Opus 5.5 at every effort level from medium to max, although at each model's own default Fable leads. The creator tests charted in the cost section below priced it similarly: Fable cost roughly 1.5 to 1.9 times Opus 5.5.

That does not make Fable pointless. Until Fable 5.2's release on November 9, 2026, Anthropic's [choosing-a-model guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/choosing-a-model) described Fable 5.1 as its most capable model open to all customers, aimed at agent sessions that run for hours, multistep research and finished documents, spreadsheets or decks, and the [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) puts Fable 5.1's cache-read price at $0.25 per MTok, a quarter of Fable 5's.

The catch is access: Pro runs Fable on usage credits only, and Max and Ultra include it for up to half the weekly limit, after which you can keep going on usage credits or switch models. During the Fable 5.2 launch offer, from November 9 through November 16, 2026, Pro also includes every Fable model for up to half the weekly limit.

Anthropic's [newsroom](https://www.anthropic.com/news) listed no Fable model newer than Fable 5.1 until November 9, 2026, when it announced Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2; the comparison below predates that release.

**Verdict:** run Opus 5.5 as the default. Fable 5.1 is a hypothesis to test on a sample of the tasks Opus at xhigh or max effort has missed, and keep it only if the result justifies the bill. We found no independent test of Fable's multi-hour research strengths, so the premium is hard to judge from public data.

## Effort Is Often a Better Lever Than Switching Tiers

Anthropic's guide says it plainly: "Tuning effort is often a better lever than switching models." Every current tier supports five effort levels (low, medium, high, xhigh and max), and the [effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort) list the API defaults: high for Fable 5.1 and Sonnet 5.5, medium for Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5.

Opus 5.5 therefore runs one level lighter than Opus 5 when a request omits effort. Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 always use adaptive thinking, so effort is the main control over how much thinking they do on complex work. Per [Anthropic's Sonnet 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5), Claude Code and the Claude apps default Sonnet 5.5 to medium.

The independent numbers show how much that setting matters. Artificial Analysis scores each model at every effort level on GDPval-AA v2.1, a test of real professional tasks, and its [live board](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gdpval-aa) was captured on November 6, 2026.

Read across a row and the scores swing by hundreds of points. Opus 5.5 spans 626 points from low to max, while the tiers at max effort sit within 235 points of each other. The order of the tiers also changes with the setting.

At their API defaults, Fable 5.1 leads (1644), then Opus 5.5 at medium (1593) and Sonnet 5.5 at high (1557). At max effort, Opus 5.5 (1866) and Sonnet 5.5 (1838) are close, with confidence intervals of about ±26 and ±23, and both beat Fable 5.1 (1763).

Higher effort is not free, and it is not always the cheaper lever. Effort changes how long the model works and how many tokens it spends. Changing the top-level effort between requests also invalidates the prompt cache, per the [effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort), while a per-message effort change preserves it on models that support it.

Anthropic's [Sonnet 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5) says the model "complements Opus 5.5 best when running at lower effort settings, where it costs less per task." It also flags a ceiling: on FrontierCode, Sonnet 5.5 scores lower at max effort than at xhigh because it more often ran a code-review skill that splits the review across many subagents, which led to timeouts or extra edits in the cases examined. Sonnet's discount also comes with a quality gap: on the same board it trails Opus by 58 Elo at low effort and 264 at medium (1329 against 1593), so the saving only holds where the task is well specified enough to tolerate that. Raise effort one level at a time, and past xhigh price out a tier switch.

Other modifiers stack on top, according to the [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing): batch processing takes 50% off input and output for work that can wait, Opus 5.5 fast mode costs $8 / $40 per MTok (twice the standard rate) and cannot be combined with batch, and US-only inference adds a 1.1x multiplier.

Output tokens carry five times the weight of input tokens on every tier, so a model that talks less is cheaper at the same rate.

Per-token prices only tell part of the story: in the Terminal-Bench runs above, token volume mattered as much as price, and the creator tests show how the bill can land on single projects.

Anthropic's choosing-a-model guide describes mixing tiers rather than picking one, with two patterns: an executor that escalates hard decisions to a stronger advisor, and an orchestrator that hands bulk work to cheaper workers, so that most tokens are billed at the lower rate.

In practice that can mean Haiku 5.5 for triage and search, Sonnet 5.5 for the main build, and Opus 5.5 for planning and review. For how the 1M window and auto-compaction work in Claude Code, see our [Claude Code context window guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-code-context-window).

Before version 2.1.280, Pro and Team Standard defaulted to Sonnet 5. If your Pro sessions drain faster than they used to, check which model is selected before blaming the plan.

Anthropic does not publish how many hours of Opus you get. Its [usage limits article](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11647753-how-do-usage-and-length-limits-work) says paid plans have a session limit that resets every five hours (except verified Ultra accounts) plus weekly limits, that usage depends on the model and the effort level, and that in the Claude apps, where Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 have no thinking toggle, a lower effort level is the way to spend less.

The [Opus 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) also announced higher five-hour usage limits on Pro, Max, Team and seat-based Enterprise plans.

## Three Checks Before You Trust Any Claude Comparison

Claude pages go stale within weeks, and most of the errors repeat. Run these three checks on any comparison, including this one.

1. **Which Haiku is it?** Haiku 5.5 launched on October 7, 2026. Haiku 4.5 ($1 / $5 per MTok on the [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)) is the previous model, so a page that lists it as the current small tier predates October.
2. **Is there one date, one benchmark version and one effort level per number?** Opus 5.5 scored 1846 on GDPval-AA v2.1 in [Anthropic's launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) and 1866 at max effort on [Artificial Analysis's board](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gdpval-aa) on November 6, so a score only means something with its source and date.
3. **What does it say about context?** Every current tier has a 1M-token window on the API. One Google AI Overview we captured on November 6, 2026 listed Haiku 5.5 at 200K tokens, which the models overview contradicts.

## Older Claude Models Still in Circulation

The previous generation has not gone away. Anthropic's [deprecations page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations) lists Opus 5, Sonnet 5 and Haiku 4.5 as active. Sonnet 4.5 is the exception: it was deprecated on September 30, 2026, and its retirement date is November 30, 2026 on the Claude API and Anthropic's other platforms, while Amazon Bedrock and the Google-managed Claude models on Google Cloud set their own dates.

Opus 5 still costs $5 / $25 per MTok on the [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), so Opus 5.5 at $4 / $20 is cheaper, while Sonnet 5 stays at $2 / $10, the same as Sonnet 5.5.

That means Opus 5.5 still costs twice as much as Sonnet 5. For the earlier models in depth, see our pages on [Claude Opus 5](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-opus-5) and [Claude Sonnet 5](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-sonnet-5).

Aliases can also leave you on an older model than you expect. In Claude Code, `opus`, `sonnet` and `haiku` resolve to the 5.5 models on the Anthropic API, but elsewhere they can land on older ones: Sonnet 4.6 and Haiku 4.5 on Claude Platform on AWS, Sonnet 4.5 and Haiku 4.5 on Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud's Agent Platform, and Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.5 and Haiku 4.5 on Microsoft Foundry.

Pin the full model name, such as `claude-opus-5-5`, when the version matters, and check any setup where `sonnet` still points to Sonnet 4.5 before that model's retirement. Some API changes also affect code written for older models: per Anthropic's [release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview), thinking cannot be disabled on Opus 5.5 (Sonnet 5.5 uses a `between_tools` setting instead of `disabled`), and forced tool use (`tool_choice` of type `any` or `tool`) returns an error on Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Fable 5.1.

One more Claude Code behavior can change the model under you. Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 run with safety classifiers that most often flag cybersecurity and biology content, and the same docs say a flagged request re-runs on a fallback model: Opus 5 or Opus 4.8 for Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5, depending on the category, and Sonnet 5 for cybersecurity requests on Sonnet 5.5 (biology-flagged Sonnet 5.5 requests end in a refusal).

The session then continues on the fallback model until you run `/model`, so a surprise model switch can be a safeguard rather than a setting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Claude model is best for writing?

Sonnet 5.5 is a sensible default and Opus 5.5 the upgrade for long pieces and vague briefs. Anthropic publishes no writing benchmark that separates them, but its [Opus 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) says early testers found Opus's writing clearer and easier to follow than earlier models, and Haiku 5.5 is enough for short, repetitive text.

### Does Opus use more of my usage than Sonnet?

Yes, by Anthropic's own account, though it publishes no exact ratio. Its Claude Code usage article says Opus "costs several times more per turn than Sonnet" and "uses meaningfully more of your quota" ([models, usage and limits in Claude Code](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14552983-models-usage-and-limits-in-claude-code)), and its [Academy guide](https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/choosing-the-right-claude-model) ranks them: "Haiku is the lightest, Sonnet is moderate, Opus is heavy, and Fable uses the most." Per turn and per finished task are different measures: in the Terminal-Bench runs above, Sonnet used more than twice the tokens of Opus.

### Which is faster, Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5?

Sonnet 5.5. Anthropic's [models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview) labels Sonnet 5.5 as fast and Opus 5.5 as moderate. For Opus 5.5, fast mode raises output speed at a premium, $8 / $40 per MTok instead of $4 / $20, according to the [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing).

### Which Opus model is best for coding?

Opus 5.5. In [Anthropic's launch table](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) it scores 66.4% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 against 52.3% for Opus 5, and 54.4% against 48.0% on FrontierCode 1.1, at $4 / $20 per MTok instead of $5 / $25.
