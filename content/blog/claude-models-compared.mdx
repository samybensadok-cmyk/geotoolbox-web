Sonnet vs Opus is the common question, but Claude's current lineup has more tiers than that. As of November 9, 2026, the lineup is **Fable 5.2**, **Opus 5.5**, **Sonnet 5.5** and **Haiku 5.5**, and each has a 1M-token context window on the API. Fable 5.2, launched that day, is the top tier for the hardest long-running work, Opus 5.5 the default for complex agentic work, Sonnet 5.5 the faster everyday model and Haiku 5.5 the cheap high-volume one.

Anthropic's own guide says to start most workloads on Opus 5.5, while its Claude Code help page calls Sonnet "the right choice for the large majority of coding work." Our reading of that tension: start Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort on well-specified work you can check yourself. Use Opus 5.5 when the goal is vague or the work runs for hours, Haiku 5.5 for volume and subagents, and Fable 5.2 when Opus still falls short at xhigh or max effort; on the hours-long agent and research work Anthropic built it for, test it alongside Opus. Try a different effort level before you change the tier.

Haiku 5.5 is the only tier priced by prompt length: Anthropic's [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) charges the higher rate on any request whose prompt is over 100,000 tokens, and the count includes cache reads and writes. The others include the full 1M window at the standard rate.

Fable 5.1, the top tier until November 9, 2026, remains available. It still costs $10 / $50 per MTok with a $0.25 cache read, the [deprecations page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations) lists it as active until at least September 1, 2027, and claude.com now files it under legacy models.

Launch dates come from Anthropic's launch posts and [API release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview), which date the Fable 5.2 release to November 9, 2026.

**Mythos 5.2** is not a fifth tier you can pick. Anthropic describes [Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) as the same underlying model with different safeguards, and organizations apply for Mythos through Anthropic's cyber and life sciences verification programs.

## Sonnet vs Opus: What Separates Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5

Opus 5.5 costs exactly twice as much per token as Sonnet 5.5 ($4 / $20 against $2 / $10), and on most coding benchmarks the two sit within a few points of each other. Anthropic's own [Sonnet 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5) says the difference shows up elsewhere: "Opus 5.5 remains clearly stronger at complex, open-ended work requiring sustained judgment."

Read the first two rows together. Anthropic's table has Sonnet ahead on Terminal-Bench and the [public leaderboard](https://www.tbench.ai/) has Opus ahead, and the error margins (a ±2.6 point standard error for Opus, 95% confidence intervals of about ±3 points on the board) mean neither gap clearly separates them. Treat the pair as roughly tied. The board itself moved to [Terminal-Bench 4.1](https://www.tbench.ai/news/terminal-bench-4-1) by November 9, 2026: it re-graded the saved 4.0 runs with hardened task checkers, and every score stayed within its earlier 95% confidence interval, while Anthropic's launch tables still report 4.0.

Cost is the more useful signal. The leaderboard's job pages for [the Sonnet run](https://hub.harborframework.com/jobs/0e520a82-ad44-56be-b1bd-ff351c5bd923) and [the Opus run](https://hub.harborframework.com/jobs/96ad98a5-70e6-5e7b-bd1e-67eecfbc644c) show that both started on September 29, before Anthropic [halved Sonnet 5.5's cache-read price](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5) on October 7, and that each ran at a 97% cache-hit rate.

At that date cache reads cost $0.20 per MTok on both models, so Sonnet was not half price on most of its tokens. It also used more than twice as many (19.4B tokens against 8.0B), which contributed to a bill about 55% higher than Opus's at September 29 prices.

Three tests published on September 28 and 29, right after the Sonnet 5.5 launch, add hands-on evidence, with one caveat: they predate the October 7 Sonnet cache-read cut, which Anthropic says makes Sonnet around 20% cheaper on most agentic work.

Nate Herk ran seven workflows at high effort and found Opus cost "about $16 more" in total and ran about 46 minutes longer, which he notes is not double the cost. In [his video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7eo-11K2e3c) he reads Anthropic's guidance this way: "if you have a task with an objective definition of done, then try that out with Sonnet 5.5 first."

The other builds show how much the bill depends on the project. In [Leon van Zyl's mini-golf test](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NzyP4VjkrvM), run at extra-high (xhigh) effort, Sonnet 5.5 cost about $26 against about $27 for Opus 5.5, and he sided with Opus: "I would much rather prefer Opus 5.5." In [Brock Mesarich's voxel-world build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn08Kdp998Y), with every model at high effort, Sonnet cost $9.02 against $11.95, and Brock called Sonnet's world "the best overall one." The creators split, and each ran a single prompt, so read them as examples, not rules.

In Claude Code, the `opusplan` alias plans on Opus and executes on Sonnet, per the [model docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config). For what each model does to your bill, see our guide to [reducing Claude Code token costs](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/reduce-claude-code-token-costs).

**Verdict:** Sonnet 5.5 is the cost-saving candidate for well-specified work you can check, started at medium effort, where Anthropic says it costs less per task. At higher effort its edge fades ("At higher settings, it can perform comparably at a similar cost"), and for ambiguous or long-running work the evidence backs Opus 5.5. Compare completed-task cost and quality on your own workload before you standardize on either.

## Haiku 5.5 vs Sonnet 5.5: When the Small Model Is Enough

Haiku 5.5 is cheap enough to change how you build, but it is not a drop-in replacement for Sonnet. Anthropic positions it for quick and repetitive workloads such as summaries, compactions, database queries and classification, and says it pairs well with Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 as a subagent, in the [Haiku 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5).

The benchmark gap explains why. In that post's table, Haiku 5.5 scores 39.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 against 70.6% for Sonnet 5.5, and 1620 against 1840 on GDPval-AA v2.1. On FrontierCode 1.1 (Main) the gap is smaller: 46.4% against Sonnet's 52.1% at xhigh effort.

Anthropic's choosing-a-model guide even offers an efficiency-first path that starts a project on Haiku 5.5 and upgrades only where it falls short, and price is why.

At $0.10 / $0.50 per MTok for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, Haiku costs one-twentieth of Sonnet 5.5's input and output rates, and Anthropic says it costs around 75% less to run than Haiku 4.5 on average (list prices for prompts up to 100K tokens fell 90%).

That changes the economics of subagents: a fleet of workers can read files, search and summarize while a bigger model makes the decisions.

Two limits matter. The price steps up to $0.50 / $2.50 once a prompt passes 100,000 tokens, which sharply cuts Haiku's savings on large prompts even though it stays cheaper per token than Sonnet.

And it is a weaker checker: a [Reddit user who reran a set of project tasks as subagents](https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeCode/comments/1x1e7ts/i_tested_haiku_55_sonnet_55_and_opus_55_as_subagents_on_6_real_tasks/) found Haiku close to Opus on code search (44 of 48 files against 45, at $0.44 against $6.17) but marking 30 correct sentences as errors while fact-checking two blog posts, against 6 for Sonnet and 0 for Opus.

It is one person's test at high effort, but it fits the pattern: let the small model fetch and sort, and let a stronger one verify.

**Verdict:** use Haiku 5.5 for classification, extraction, routing, search and subagent legwork under 100K tokens per request, and keep Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5 on consequential judgments and final verification.

## Fable 5.2 vs Opus 5.5: Is the Top Tier Worth the Premium?

Fable 5.2 costs $8 / $40 per MTok, twice Opus 5.5 and four times Sonnet 5.5, with the same $0.20 cache-read price as Opus 5.5, and Anthropic's guide still starts most workloads on Opus 5.5.

Its [choosing-a-model guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/choosing-a-model) says most workloads start with Opus 5.5, and that if your evals "at xhigh or max effort still fall short on demanding reasoning or long-horizon agentic work," you move to Claude Fable 5.2.

Anthropic's November 9 launch table puts Fable 5.2 above Opus 5.5 on most rows, reversing the Fable 5.1 result. The [Fable 5.2 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) calls it "a step above Opus 5.5 in almost all the capabilities we evaluated," with each model at its highest available effort unless noted. In the [Opus 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) of September 22, Fable 5.1 trailed Opus 5.5 on every row.

Opus 5.5 still leads on AA-Briefcase's Elo score (1800 against 1785), a knowledge-work test, although Fable 5.2 leads on its rubric score (66.1% against 61.2%). These are also not the same runs as the September 22 post, which put Opus at 40.0% on AutomationBench rather than 42.5%, so compare figures within one table rather than across posts.

Anthropic's [launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) says that in early testing on FrontierCode 2.0, Fable 5.2 at high effort scored about the same as Opus 5.5's best score at max effort (45.3% against 45.5%) "for roughly half the cost per task." It adds that at medium effort, its default in the Claude apps and Claude Code, Fable 5.2 finishes hard tasks with fewer tokens than Fable 5.1, and that it generates output up to 30% faster.

The first independent scores, which arrived on launch day, are mixed. Artificial Analysis's [GDPval-AA board](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gdpval-aa) scored Fable 5.2 at every effort level by the evening of November 9, 2026 (UTC). At each model's API default, Fable 5.2 (high, 1718) leads Opus 5.5 (medium, 1593).

At the same effort level, the result depends on the setting. Fable 5.2 is far ahead at low (1546 against 1240) and level at medium (1591 against 1593) and high (1718 against 1714). It is clearly behind at xhigh (1751 against 1835, with margins of about ±21 and ±23 that do not overlap) and slightly behind at max (1831 against 1866), where the margins overlap. The [Terminal-Bench leaderboard](https://www.tbench.ai/) had no Fable 5.2 run yet.

For Fable 5.1 the independent data is fuller, and Opus 5.5 comes out ahead: on the Terminal-Bench 4.1 re-grade, Fable 5.1's best run scores 57.6% at xhigh against 65.8% for Opus 5.5 at max, and the Artificial Analysis board in the next section has Fable 5.1 under Opus 5.5 at every effort level from medium to max. Our [Fable 5.1 guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-fable-5-1) covers the previous model in depth.

Anthropic's guide calls Fable 5.2 its most capable model open to all customers and points it at agent sessions that run for hours, multistep deep research, and analysis carried through to a finished document, spreadsheet or deck. The launch post also suggests using it as an advisor that Haiku, Sonnet or Opus models consult on harder problems.

Access depends on your plan and, through November 16, 2026, on the Fable 5.2 launch offer (see the plan section). Fable 5.2 also requires 30-day data retention and is not available under zero data retention unless Anthropic expressly authorizes it, per its [What's new page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-2/whats-new-fable-5-2).

**Verdict:** run Opus 5.5 as the default. Fable 5.2 is cheaper than Fable 5.1 and, on most rows of Anthropic's own table, stronger than Opus 5.5, but the first independent board only partly agrees: there Fable 5.2 leads Opus at each model's default effort, is level at medium and high, trails clearly at xhigh and sits inside the margin at max. Test it on a sample of your hardest tasks, including ones Opus at xhigh or max has missed, and compare total cost, quality and time on completed tasks: list prices are twice Opus's, but the bill per task can differ. We found no independent test yet of the multi-hour agent and research work Anthropic aims it at.

## Effort Is Often a Better Lever Than Switching Tiers

Anthropic's guide says it plainly: "Tuning effort is often a better lever than switching models." Every current tier supports five effort levels (low, medium, high, xhigh and max), and the [effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort) list the API defaults: high for Fable 5.2 and Sonnet 5.5, medium for Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5.

Opus 5 defaulted to high, so Opus 5.5 runs one level lighter when a request omits effort. Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 always use adaptive thinking, so effort is the main control over how much thinking they do on complex work. Per [Anthropic's Sonnet 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5), Claude Code and the Claude apps default Sonnet 5.5 to medium, and the [Fable 5.2 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) says the same of Fable 5.2. The effort docs advise starting Fable 5.2 at high and running your own effort sweep rather than carrying settings over from Fable 5.1.

The independent numbers show how much that setting matters. Artificial Analysis scores each model at every effort level on GDPval-AA v2.1, a test of real professional tasks. Its [live board](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gdpval-aa) was captured on November 6, 2026 and again on November 9, when it had added Fable 5.2 at every effort level; the earlier rows were unchanged.

Read across a row and the scores swing by hundreds of points. Opus 5.5 spans 626 points from low to max, while the tiers at max effort sit within 235 points of each other. The order of the tiers also changes with the setting.

At their API defaults, Fable 5.2 leads (1718 at high), ahead of Fable 5.1 (1644), Opus 5.5 at medium (1593) and Sonnet 5.5 at high (1557). Higher effort is not free, and it is not always the cheaper lever. Effort changes how long the model works and how many tokens it spends. Changing the top-level effort between requests also invalidates the prompt cache, per the [effort docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort), while a per-message effort change preserves it on models that support it.

Anthropic's [Sonnet 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5) says the model "complements Opus 5.5 best when running at lower effort settings, where it costs less per task." It also flags a ceiling: on FrontierCode, Sonnet 5.5 scores lower at max effort than at xhigh because it more often ran a code-review skill that splits the review across many subagents, which led to timeouts or extra edits in the cases examined. Sonnet's discount also comes with a quality gap. On the same board it trails Opus by 58 Elo at low effort and 264 at medium (1329 against 1593), or by 36 at each model's API default (1557 against 1593).

The saving only holds where the task is well specified enough to tolerate that gap. Raise effort one level at a time, and past xhigh price out a tier switch.

## Cost per Task, Not Cost per Million Tokens

The rate card tells you what one request costs. A task is many requests, and each model decides how many it needs. Here is the same single request priced on every tier, using the November 9 rates from Anthropic's [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing): 80,000 input tokens and 8,000 output tokens, with and without 60,000 of the input served from the prompt cache.

Our arithmetic, from list prices: the spread between the current tiers is 1x, 20x, 40x and 80x without caching (100x for Fable 5.1), and caching cuts every bill by roughly 45% to 49%, before counting the 1.25x price of writing the cache on the first request.

Other modifiers stack on top, according to the [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing): batch processing takes 50% off input and output for work that can wait, fast mode costs twice the standard rate ($8 / $40 per MTok on Opus 5.5, $16 / $80 on Fable 5.2) and cannot be combined with batch, and US-only inference adds a 1.1x multiplier.

Output tokens carry five times the weight of input tokens on every tier, so a model that talks less is cheaper at the same rate.

Per-token prices only tell part of the story: in the Terminal-Bench runs above, token volume mattered as much as price, and the creator tests show how the bill can land on single projects.

Anthropic's choosing-a-model guide describes mixing tiers rather than picking one, with two patterns: an executor that escalates hard decisions to a stronger advisor, and an orchestrator that hands bulk work to cheaper workers, so that most tokens are billed at the lower rate.

## Three Checks Before You Trust Any Claude Comparison

Claude pages go stale within weeks, and most of the errors repeat. Run these three checks on any comparison, including this one.

1. **Which Haiku is it?** Haiku 5.5 launched on October 7, 2026. Haiku 4.5 ($1 / $5 per MTok on the [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)) is the previous model, so a page that lists it as the current small tier predates October.
2. **Is there one date, one benchmark version and one effort level per number?** Opus 5.5 scored 1846 on GDPval-AA v2.1 in [Anthropic's launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) and 1866 at max effort on [Artificial Analysis's board](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gdpval-aa) on November 6, so a score only means something with its source and date.
3. **What does it say about context?** Every current tier has a 1M-token window on the API. Google AI Overviews we captured on November 6 and November 9, 2026 listed Haiku 5.5 at 200K tokens, which the models overview contradicts.

## Older Claude Models Still in Circulation

The previous generation has not gone away. Anthropic's [deprecations page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations) lists Fable 5.1, Opus 5, Sonnet 5 and Haiku 4.5 as active. Sonnet 4.5 is the exception: it was deprecated on September 30, 2026, and its retirement date is November 30, 2026 on the Claude API and Anthropic's other platforms, while Amazon Bedrock and the Google-managed Claude models on Google Cloud set their own dates.

Opus 5 still costs $5 / $25 per MTok on the [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), so Opus 5.5 at $4 / $20 is cheaper, while Sonnet 5 stays at $2 / $10, the same as Sonnet 5.5.

That means Opus 5.5 still costs twice as much as Sonnet 5. Against the oldest models, list prices also understate the bill: the pricing page says Claude 4.7 and later models use a newer tokenizer that produces approximately 30% more tokens for the same text, while Sonnet 4.6 and earlier use the previous one. Moving a prompt from Haiku 4.5 or Sonnet 4.5 to a 5.5 model therefore bills more tokens for the same words. For the earlier models in depth, see our pages on [Claude Opus 5](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-opus-5) and [Claude Sonnet 5](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/claude-sonnet-5).

Aliases can also leave you on an older model than you expect. In Claude Code, `opus`, `sonnet` and `haiku` resolve to the 5.5 models on the Anthropic API, but elsewhere they can land on older ones: Sonnet 4.6 and Haiku 4.5 on Claude Platform on AWS, Sonnet 4.5 and Haiku 4.5 on Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud's Agent Platform, and Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.5 and Haiku 4.5 on Microsoft Foundry.

The `fable` alias moved on launch day. It now resolves to Fable 5.2 everywhere except Claude apps gateway sessions, where it means Fable 5, and Fable 5.2 needs Claude Code v2.1.321 or later, per the [model configuration docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config). A `claude-fable-5-1` setting saved in your user settings moves to the `fable` alias automatically on Pro and Max; on Team and Enterprise the move waits for your organization's settings, and Console accounts, third-party providers and project, local or managed settings keep Fable 5.1.

Fable 5.2 runs on the Claude API, Claude Platform on AWS, Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry, per Anthropic's [release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview). On Bedrock, AWS's [model card](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-anthropic-claude-fable-5-2.html) lists `anthropic.claude-fable-5-2` with US and global inference only and an opt-in to AWS review data retention.

Pin the full model name, such as `claude-opus-5-5`, when the version matters, and check any setup where `sonnet` still points to Sonnet 4.5 before that model's retirement. Some API changes also affect code written for older models: per Anthropic's [release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview), thinking cannot be disabled on Opus 5.5 (Sonnet 5.5 uses a `between_tools` setting instead of `disabled`), and forced tool use (`tool_choice` of type `any` or `tool`) returns an error on Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Fable 5.1 and Fable 5.2.

Fable 5.2 adds changes of its own, per the same notes and Anthropic's [What's new in Fable 5.2](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-2/whats-new-fable-5-2) page. It enforces the prompt prefix check on every account, and outside Amazon Bedrock it supports computer use only through the `computer_toolset_20260801` toolset. In the advisor tool, every executor model accepts Fable 5.2 as its advisor, but a Fable 5.2 executor accepts only Fable 5.2 or Mythos 5.2.

One more Claude Code behavior can change the model under you. Fable models, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5 run with safety classifiers that most often flag cybersecurity and biology content, and the [model configuration docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config) say a flagged request re-runs on a fallback model. On Fable models and Opus 5.5, biology requests re-run on Opus 5 and cybersecurity requests on Opus 4.8. Sonnet 5.5 falls back to Sonnet 5 and Opus 5 to Opus 4.8 for cybersecurity requests, and biology-flagged requests on those two end in a refusal.

The session then continues on the fallback model until you run `/model`, so a surprise model switch can be a safeguard rather than a setting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Claude model is best for writing?

Sonnet 5.5 is a sensible default and Opus 5.5 the upgrade for long pieces and vague briefs. Anthropic publishes no writing benchmark that separates them, but its [Opus 5.5 launch post](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) says early testers found Opus's writing clearer and easier to follow than earlier models, and Haiku 5.5 is enough for short, repetitive text.

### Does Opus use more of my usage than Sonnet?

Yes, by Anthropic's own account, though it publishes no exact ratio. Its Claude Code usage article says Opus "costs several times more per turn than Sonnet" and "uses meaningfully more of your quota" ([models, usage and limits in Claude Code](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14552983-models-usage-and-limits-in-claude-code)), and its [Academy guide](https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/choosing-the-right-claude-model) ranks them: "Haiku is the lightest, Sonnet is moderate, Opus is heavy, and Fable uses the most." Per turn and per finished task are different measures: in the Terminal-Bench runs above, Sonnet used more than twice the tokens of Opus.

### Which is faster, Sonnet 5.5 or Opus 5.5?

Sonnet 5.5. Anthropic's [models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview) labels Sonnet 5.5 as fast and Opus 5.5 as moderate. For Opus 5.5, fast mode raises output speed at a premium, $8 / $40 per MTok instead of $4 / $20, according to the [pricing page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing).

### Which Opus model is best for coding?

Opus 5.5. In [Anthropic's launch table](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) it scores 66.4% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 against 52.3% for Opus 5, and 54.4% against 48.0% on FrontierCode 1.1, at $4 / $20 per MTok instead of $5 / $25.
