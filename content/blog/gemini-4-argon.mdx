Gemini 4 Argon is the Gemini 4 frontier model included with Google AI Pro, released on October 13, 2026. You can use it in the Gemini app with a Google AI Pro or Ultra plan, or on the API as `gemini-4-argon` at $2 input and $10 output per million tokens through November 30, 2026, then $4 and $20 from December 1.

It is not in the free or AI Plus plans, has no free API tier, and is no longer Google's top model: [Gemini 4 Carbon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/) ranks higher and, since November 10, 2026, is open to Google AI Ultra subscribers and paying API developers.

## What Is Gemini 4 Argon?

Gemini 4 Argon is Google DeepMind's frontier model and the first release of the Gemini 4 generation. Google built it for long, hard work, according to its [September 30 announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/): real-world software engineering, legal and finance tasks, and cyber defense.

It reached the public in stages:

1. **September 30, 2026:** announced as a preview for cyber defenders in Google's Fairwind Program. Google's GA post says Fairwind members can still get Argon "without cyber guardrails"; the public version keeps them.
2. **October 13, 2026:** [made globally available](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/) on the API, in the Gemini app for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, and in AI Mode in Search.

That two-week gap is why some coverage still describes Argon as unveiled but not yet released. It was written between September 30 and October 13, and one such headline was still in Google's results for "gemini 4 argon" on November 8.

Argon sits above Gemini 3.1 Pro and the Flash models in Google's lineup, and below Gemini 4 Carbon. On the Google AI Pro plan, Argon now appears next to "our Pro model" (3.1 Pro), which is still available, so you get both. Free users get a Flash-Lite model, and AI Plus now gets Gemini 4 Hydrogen, launched November 12. For the full picture of how these tiers fit, see our guide to [Google's Gemini lineup](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/what-is-gemini).

**One naming trap:** there is no "Gemini 4 Pro." People search for it because earlier generations had a Pro model, but the Gemini 4 generation uses element names, Argon and then Carbon. The model still called "Pro" in Google's apps is Gemini 3.1 Pro.

**Argon vs Gemini 3.1 Pro.** On the [Gemini API](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing), 3.1 Pro (still a preview model) costs $2 input and $12 output per million tokens for prompts up to 200K tokens, and $4 and $18 above that.

Argon has no long-prompt surcharge. Through November 30 it costs the same or less than 3.1 Pro at every prompt length; from December 1 it costs more at every prompt length (above 200K tokens the input rate is the same $4; output is $20 vs $18).

Argon's knowledge cutoff is March 2026, per the [Gemini 4 model card](https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-4-argon/), so it will not know about later events unless it searches. It also has low, medium and high thinking levels, which change both answer quality and how fast you use up your limits.

**Antigravity changed after launch.** On October 13, Google's GA post listed Antigravity access for AI Ultra subscribers only. **AI Mode has moved on.** At launch, Google's [AI Mode help page](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16011537) offered Argon to AI Pro and Ultra subscribers aged 18 and over in a list of countries that included the US, the UK and Switzerland but no European Union member state. As of November 14, 2026, that page and Google's plans page no longer list Argon for AI Mode. The help page names Gemini 4 Hydrogen, which Google's VP of Product for Search calls the new default, and Gemini 4 Carbon for Ultra subscribers.

**There are two narrow exceptions.** Antigravity's footnote also covers trials on eligible premium devices, and Google is giving the highest Antigravity usage tier to 10,000 scientists for 6 months, subject to eligibility, capacity and selection, per its [GA post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/).

## Usage Limits: Why Argon Runs Out Fast on AI Pro

One of the most common complaints about Argon is not about quality. It is how quickly it uses up the Gemini app's limit on the AI Pro plan.

Google does not publish a message count. Its [plans page footnote](https://gemini.google/us/subscriptions/) says limits are "compute-based" and "factor in the complexity of your prompt, the features you use, and the length of your chat." The limit "refreshes every 5 hours until you reach your weekly limit." AI Pro gets 4x the usage of the free plan, and AI Ultra gets 5x or 20x the usage of AI Pro, depending on the tier.

Because the meter counts compute, a heavy model on a high thinking level drains it far faster than a Flash model. Pro subscribers have posted what that looks like in practice:

- "Literally let it print 'This is a test' and used 2% of my 5 hour limit," wrote the author of [a 582-upvote r/GeminiAI thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/GeminiAI/comments/1xd6kze/whats_wrong_with_argons_usage_limit) in late October.
- Another Pro user in the same thread said one short stock-market question on high thinking "used 100% of my fresh 5 hour usage limit."

These are reported experiences, not measurements, and other users in the same threads report milder usage. They still match the mechanism Google describes.

The first lever is the thinking level. Argon offers low, medium and high.

On the API and on Google Cloud, [medium is the default](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/gemini/4-argon); in the Gemini app, Google's [help page](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/18648722) says it defaults to low if you do not choose. The same page says "higher thinking levels require more of your usage," so keeping high for the hard questions should help a Pro allowance last longer, though by how much depends on the task. One of the most-upvoted replies in that thread was simply "switch from high to medium."

If you regularly hit the cap on Pro, drop the thinking level, move routine work to a lighter model (Gemini 3.1 Pro or Gemini 4 Hydrogen, both included on Pro), buy AI credits, which the plans page says extend your limits, or pay for Ultra. Developers can skip the app limits entirely and pay per token on the API.

Three billing details matter more than the headline rate:

- **Thinking tokens are billed as output.** The output price explicitly includes them, so a high thinking level raises your bill as well as your latency.
- **Context caching is implicit only.** Google's [pricing page](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing) notes that explicit caching is not supported for Argon, so you cannot pin a prompt prefix; you get the discount when the system detects a repeat.
- **Search grounding is extra.** It includes 5,000 free requests a month, shared across Gemini 3 and newer models, then $14 per 1,000, with a daily cap of 1,500 requests.

A quick worked example: an app that sends 10 million input tokens and receives 2 million output tokens a month pays $40 at the introductory rate ($20 + $20). From December 1, the same traffic costs $80. If your budget was set in October, plan for that.

Against rivals, Argon moves from GPT-6.1 Sol's price point to Claude Opus 5.5's. Through November 30 it costs the same $2/$10 as [GPT-6.1 Sol](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing) for prompts up to 272K input tokens; OpenAI charges more above that, while Argon keeps one rate across its whole window. From December 1 it matches [Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) at $4/$20.

List price understates the cost, because Argon uses a lot of tokens. [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-4-argon) reports that Argon on high used 113 million tokens to run its Intelligence Index, against a median of 72.6 million, and calls it "notably slow": 27.7 minutes per task against a 12.6-minute median.

Artificial Analysis prices Argon at the $4/$20 standard rate, which puts Argon (high) at $9.95 per task on its [leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models).

That is about the same as Claude Opus 5.5 at max effort ($10.04), its best-scoring setting, and far above Opus 5.5 on high ($3.46). At the introductory $2/$10 rate, the same token use would cost roughly half. ### Hallucination

On Artificial Analysis's AA-Omniscience test, Argon (high) has the [lowest hallucination rate](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-4-argon) of the models on its default chart. That metric counts wrong answers among all of a model's non-correct responses (wrong, partial or not attempted), so Argon rarely answers wrongly when it does not get a question right.

Its accuracy on the same test is lower than Carbon's, Opus 5.5's or GPT-6.1 Sol's, so a low hallucination rate here does not mean it knows more.

## Where Argon Is Strong, and Where It Falls Short

Argon earns its price on long, multi-step work where most of the effort is reading and reasoning over a lot of material. Beyond the benchmark leads above, these strengths stand out:

- **Very long outputs.** By default a response stops at 65,536 tokens. With long decode and [background execution](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/latest-model), the model generates in hops of up to 64K tokens, each continuing where the last one stopped, so a single response can run to roughly 1M tokens minus your input. That suits large refactors, full reports and big code migrations.
- **Agentic coding in Antigravity.** Google's [GA post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/) describes Argon helping the Chrome team separate code out of a roughly 6-million-line legacy folder. Treat vendor case studies as a ceiling, not an average.
- **Prompt injection resistance.** Google reported a 0.7% attack success rate for Argon on Gray Swan's Indirect Prompt Injection benchmark, the best result until [Carbon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-carbon/) posted 0.0%. That matters if you let it browse or read email.

The weak spots follow from the same design:

- **Speed.** Artificial Analysis measured 27.7 minutes per index task, more than twice the median.
- **Cost per task.** Its verbosity puts it at about $9.95 per task on Artificial Analysis's board at the December list price (roughly half at the introductory rate), and it drains the app's usage limit quickly.
- **Agentic coding.** It trails on FrontierSWE and Terminal-Bench, so shell-driven agents may do better elsewhere.
- **Fresh knowledge.** The March 2026 cutoff means anything newer needs search grounding.

If your work is mostly short questions, you will pay for capability you do not use.

## How to Access Gemini 4 Argon

There are several ways in, depending on whether you want a chat app or an API:

1. **Gemini app (web, Android, iOS, macOS):** subscribe to AI Pro or Ultra, then pick Gemini 4 Argon in the model menu.
2. **AI Mode in Google Search:** Google's help page no longer lists Argon there (checked November 14, 2026); Ultra subscribers can pick Carbon from the menu in the "Ask anything" bar instead. See our explainer on [how Google AI Mode works](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/what-is-google-ai-mode).
3. **Google Antigravity:** open the model picker in Google's agentic development platform with a paid AI Pro or an Ultra plan.
4. **Gemini API and Google AI Studio:** call the model ID `gemini-4-argon` on a paid API project; the [model page](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/models/gemini-4-argon) lists limits and thinking settings.
5. **Google Cloud or a router:** use Google Cloud's [Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/gemini/4-argon) for enterprise controls, or third-party routers such as [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/google/gemini-4-argon).

## What Argon Means for Your Brand in AI Search

Gemini models also answer in Google Search. From October 13, AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in supported countries could switch AI Mode answers to Argon, an option Google's help page no longer lists (November 14); since November 12, AI Mode's default is Gemini 4 Hydrogen, per Google's VP of Product for Search, and Carbon is the Ultra option. Those answers are part of the same search journey your customers already use, and they cite sources.

What we at geotoolbox do not know yet is whether Argon picks different sources than AI Mode's default model. We have not tested that, and Google has not said. Comparisons like [Grok vs Gemini](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/grok-vs-gemini) show how much citation behavior differs between engines, so it is worth checking rather than assuming.

The step is the same for every new model: check whether AI answers mention and cite your brand for the questions you care about, and keep checking as models change. geotoolbox's [AI Visibility Tracker](https://geotoolbox.ai/features/ai-visibility-tracker) runs your prompts through Gemini and Google AI Mode, among other engines, and records who is named and cited. Starter's fixed engines do not include those two; on Plus and Growth you can add them as part of your engine picks, and Pro and Scale run every engine.

Treat any tracker's Gemini and AI Mode results, ours included, as what a default user sees: the premium models only answer when a signed-in subscriber picks them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Gemini 4 Argon better than Claude Opus 5.5?
It depends on the job. Argon ranks above Opus 5.5 on the [Arena text board](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text) (1522 vs 1506 on November 8, 2026) and leads on Google's legal, finance and long-context tests, while Opus 5.5 leads on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and scores higher on the [Artificial Analysis index](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models) (59 at max effort vs 50 for Argon on high, each at its best setting). From December 1, 2026, both cost $4 input and $20 output per million tokens on the API, per [Google](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing) and [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing).

### Is Gemini 4 Argon better than ChatGPT?
It depends on what you measure. Comparing Argon with GPT-6.1 Sol, the OpenAI model at Argon's price point: on the [Arena text board](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text) Argon (1522) sits well above GPT-6.1 Sol at max effort (1482), so people prefer its answers. On the [Artificial Analysis index](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models), GPT-6.1 Sol at max effort scores 55 to Argon's 50 and costs $1.27 per task against $9.95. GPT-6.1 Sol's [API list price](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing) is $2 input and $10 output per million tokens for prompts up to 272K tokens, the same as Argon's introductory price through November 30, 2026.

### Can I use Gemini 4 Argon in the EU?
In the Gemini app, yes: Google rolled Argon out globally on October 13, 2026 for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. In AI Mode in Search, no: Google's [AI Mode help page](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16011537) listed no EU member state for Argon on November 8, 2026, and by November 14 it no longer listed Argon at all.
