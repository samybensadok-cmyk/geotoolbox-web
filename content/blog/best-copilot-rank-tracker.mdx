## What a Copilot Rank Tracker Actually Tracks

Start with the thing most tool pages skip: [Microsoft Copilot](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/what-is-copilot) does not rank pages in a numbered list. It reads a question, grounds the answer in Bing's search index, and writes a few sentences that name and cite a handful of sources. So a "Copilot rank tracker" is not tracking a rank. It is tracking whether Copilot mentions you, cites you, and how it describes you, across a set of prompts run again and again.

That splits into a small family of things the useful tools measure, and you should know which you are buying:

- **Brand mentions** are when Copilot names you in the answer text, with or without a link. They shape perception.
- **Citations** are the linked sources Copilot attributes the answer to. They can drive referral clicks.
- [**Share of voice**](https://geotoolbox.ai/glossary/share-of-voice) is how often you appear against named competitors across the same prompt set.
- **Sentiment** is how Copilot frames you when it does mention you, which matters as much as whether it does.

There is one more reason position matters here. Copilot runs one of the tightest citation budgets of any major assistant. In the citation database published by Omnia, one of the trackers compared below (so weight its numbers as a vendor's own), Copilot averages 4.36 distinct domains per answer, against 9.97 for ChatGPT, and six in ten Copilot answers cite four domains or fewer. Fewer slots means the gap between "cited" and "invisible" is narrow, so a tool that reports [citation](https://geotoolbox.ai/glossary/ai-citation) share and position, not just a raw [mention](https://geotoolbox.ai/glossary/brand-mention) count, is doing the more useful job. If a dashboard shows a single "visibility score" with no method behind it, treat that as marketing, not a metric.

The [same Omnia data](https://www.useomnia.com/blog/rank-tracking-tool-copilot) hides a useful pattern. Citations barely overlap between engines: only about 13% of the domains ChatGPT cites, and 18% of Perplexity's, also show up in Copilot for the same prompt. But a domain cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity appears in Copilot roughly 39.6% of the time, close to triple the 13% rate for a domain cited by ChatGPT alone. Whatever drives that overlap, being cited across engines tends to coincide with being cited in Copilot, so a tracker that shows you where you stand on the other engines gives you a useful leading signal.

## How Tracking Copilot Differs From Other Engines

Three differences change what you should expect from a tool, and the first one is good news.

**Copilot's sources overlap with regular SEO.** Web Copilot grounds its answers in the Bing index and the follow-up searches it runs on your behalf. Its citation layer sits closer to the classic web-ranking surface than ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity: it leans on pages Bing already crawls and ranks. That means the [SEO work you do for Copilot](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/copilot-seo) is more traceable than it is elsewhere: you can correlate Bing indexing with Copilot citations instead of guessing, even though any single reading stays noisy. It also means the tracking problem is more tractable here than on the [other engines we cover](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/how-to-track-ai-visibility).

**It answers from several surfaces, and they differ.** Copilot lives in Bing chat, the Edge sidebar, Windows, and the Microsoft 365 apps, and the same prompt can return different answers depending on where it runs. A tracker has to standardize which surface it samples, or its numbers drift for reasons that have nothing to do with your content. Results also shift by country, because Bing leans on regional indexes, so a global average can hide a weak home market. Ask any tool which surface and which market its data comes from before you trust a trend line.

**The same prompt gives different answers.** Copilot is non-deterministic. Ask it twice and the cited sources can change by time, phrasing, and location. In Omnia's data, only about 41.5% of Copilot prompts keep the same top-cited domain week over week, the most volatile of the major engines it measured. One check is only directional. A tracker earns its price by running a fixed prompt set on a schedule and reporting the trend, not by telling you where you sat at 3pm on a Tuesday.

## The Part Most Guides Blur: Web Copilot vs Microsoft 365 Copilot

This is the single most important thing to understand before you buy anything, and most tool pages talk around it. "Copilot" is two different products.

**Web Copilot** is the consumer assistant: Bing chat, copilot.microsoft.com, the Edge sidebar, and Copilot in Windows. It grounds on the public Bing index. This is the only Copilot that any third-party tracker can actually measure, because anyone can send it a prompt and read the answer.

**Microsoft 365 Copilot** is the work assistant embedded in Word, Excel, Outlook, and Teams. It runs inside your organization's private tenant and grounds on your own SharePoint, email, and files behind authentication, blended with web results. No outside tool can see another company's tenant answers, because the tenant is a privacy and compliance boundary by design, as [Microsoft's own documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy) on how M365 Copilot handles tenant data spells out.

Why this matters for your money: vendors love to justify a Copilot tracker by pointing at enterprise reach, and the reach is real. Microsoft reported [more than 20 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats](https://www.ciodive.com/news/microsoft-earnings-Q3-2026/819009/) in its FY26 Q3 results, its fastest quarterly jump since launch. But a tracker cannot watch how you appear inside those seats. What it measures is public web Copilot, sampled as a neutral user. That is still worth doing. Just know the boundary: M365 Copilot grounds mostly on private tenant data, so the only external lever you have is your public, Bing-indexed content, and only when Copilot's optional web-grounding step reaches past the tenant for it. Nothing a dashboard reads touches inside the tenant.

## Check This First: Can Bing Even Reach and Index You?

Before you pay for any tracker, rule out the failure that no tool can fix. Web Copilot cites the pages Bing has crawled and indexed. If [BingBot](https://geotoolbox.ai/glossary/bingbot) is blocked or your pages are not in the Bing index, there is next to nothing for a tracker to find, and the dashboard will show a flat zero that has nothing to do with your content.

Three quick checks settle it:

1. Confirm your robots.txt does not disallow `bingbot`, and that no firewall or bot filter is quietly dropping it. This is the most common self-inflicted [AI-crawler](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/ai-crawlers) block we see.
2. Verify the pages you care about are actually in Bing (a `site:` check, or URL inspection in Bing Webmaster Tools).
3. Use IndexNow to push new and changed URLs to Bing so Copilot grounds on the current version instead of a stale one.

If Copilot is not citing you, this is where to look before a tracker, not after. You can run a free reachability check with our [AI crawler checker](https://geotoolbox.ai/tools/ai-crawler-checker) to see whether the major AI and search crawlers, BingBot included, can actually fetch your pages.

## Which Tools Actually Run Copilot Live (and Which Just Say They Do)

"Supports AI search" is not the same as "tracks Copilot," and the difference is where buyers get burned. Three things separate real coverage from a checkbox.

**Live browser sessions versus API calls.** Some tools run prompts through a real browser session to capture what a person actually sees; others hit an API that can return different output. Vendors that use browser sessions, Omnia and Profound among them, argue that approach reflects reality more faithfully on a volatile engine like Copilot. They have an interest in the claim, and neither approach is automatically wrong, but you should know which one you are paying for.

**Claimed versus verified.** The clearest example is SE Ranking and its SE Visible module. One guide awards it strong Copilot coverage; Omnia's own comparison flatly states that neither SE Visible nor SE Ranking tracks Copilot directly. Both cannot be true. The takeaway is to verify native Copilot on the exact plan you intend to buy, inside a trial, before you pay, rather than trusting any single roundup.

**"Coming soon" is not coverage.** Several suites list Copilot on a roadmap. Treat a roadmap as a zero until the data shows up in your dashboard.

## Is There a Free Way to Track Copilot?

Partly, yes, and you should use it before paying for anything. Microsoft launched a free [AI Performance report in Bing Webmaster Tools](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) in February 2026. It shows total citations, average cited pages, and the grounding queries Copilot generates to retrieve content, for pages across Microsoft Copilot and Bing's AI answers. It is first-party and authoritative, with one limit: it shows your domain only, so there is no competitor share of voice.

Beyond that, Orchly runs a free one-off check, and you can always spot-check by hand: write down 15 to 25 real buyer questions, run them in Copilot on a schedule, and log the surface, date, whether you appeared, your position, the cited URLs, and which competitors showed up. Manual works for a tiny prompt set. It stops working the moment you need trends, volume, or a repeatable sample, which is the whole reason paid trackers exist.

## What to Look for in a Copilot Rank Tracker

Once you are past the reachability check and know web-Copilot is what you are measuring, a good tool clears a short bar:

- **Native Copilot, not just "AI search."** Confirm Copilot is a real, separate engine in the product, with its own numbers.
- **A stated surface.** It should tell you whether it samples Bing chat, Edge, or the Copilot app, and keep it consistent.
- **Scheduled, repeated sampling.** Copilot's volatility makes any single run only directional. You want a fixed prompt set run on a cadence, with trend history.
- **Citation and source transparency.** Not just "you were mentioned," but which pages were cited and where you sat among them.
- **Competitor share of voice.** The point of paying is seeing who wins when you lose.
- **An honest method.** Be wary of a single black-box "visibility score" with no explanation of how it is computed.

One attribution caveat to plan around: Copilot referral traffic often arrives stripped of its referrer, so it lands in GA4 as direct or unassigned. A tracker's citation data, rather than your analytics, is usually the more reliable signal that Copilot is sending you anything.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Microsoft Copilot have a rank you can track?

Not in the blue-link sense. Copilot writes a synthesized answer and cites a few sources rather than listing ranked pages. What you track is whether it mentions you, whether it cites your domain and in what position, your share of voice against competitors, and the sentiment of how it describes you, sampled across a fixed prompt set over time.

### Can you track Microsoft 365 (work) Copilot?

No, not externally. Microsoft 365 Copilot runs inside your organization's private tenant and grounds on your own files behind authentication, so no third-party tool can read another company's answers. Every third-party tracker here measures public web Copilot (Bing, Edge, Windows), sampled as a neutral user. Plan around that boundary.

### Which tools actually track Copilot live?

The dedicated AI-visibility platforms (Otterly, Peec AI, Profound, Scrunch, AIclicks, Dageno, Searchable, Omnia, and our own Geotoolbox) generally run Copilot as a real engine, though Profound gates it to its enterprise tiers. Among SEO suites, coverage varies and at least one (SE Ranking / SE Visible) has conflicting claims across guides. Trial the exact plan and confirm Copilot appears as its own engine before paying.

### Is tracking Copilot different from Bing SEO?

They are linked but not the same. Copilot grounds its answers in the Bing index, so strong Bing SEO (indexing, IndexNow, clean structure) is the pipeline that gets you eligible to be cited. Tracking then measures whether that eligibility turns into actual mentions and citations inside Copilot answers, which Bing rank alone does not tell you.

### Can I track Copilot for free?

Partly. Bing Webmaster Tools has a free AI Performance report that shows your own domain's Copilot and Bing-AI citations, and Orchly offers a free one-off check. Neither gives competitor share of voice, and manual spot-checking does not scale, which is where paid trackers earn their price.

### How often should I check Copilot?

Weekly is the floor, because Copilot is the most volatile major engine and single checks mislead. Run a fixed prompt set on a schedule and read the trend, not any one snapshot. Daily is worth it only if your category moves fast or you are actively remediating a visibility drop.
