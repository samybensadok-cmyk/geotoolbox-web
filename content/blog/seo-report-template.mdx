## What a Monthly SEO Report Should Do

**A monthly SEO report has three jobs: show results tied to business outcomes, explain what changed and why, and decide what happens next.** An SEO report is a regular summary of how a site performs in organic search (and now in AI search), written for the person who has to decide whether the work continues.

The second job is the easiest to skip. The numbers went up or down, the report says which, and it stops there. It does not say why, what it means for leads, or what you will do about it. 

The fix is structural. Put the answer first (result, cause, next step), keep the evidence one layer down, and cut every number that would not change a decision. One report then serves both the stakeholder who owns revenue and the marketing lead who wants the pages and queries behind the movement.

## How to Build Each Section

Build the sections in data order, then write the summary last. The spreadsheet follows the same order, so you can fill it in as you read.

### Executive Summary

A few short sentences: what happened, why, and what you will do next. Name one number, one cause and one action, then stop. An example of the shape:

> Organic key events rose from 30 to 36 month over month, driven by two rewritten product pages that moved into the top 3. Sessions grew more slowly because the blog lost seasonal traffic. Next month we add FAQ sections to the ten highest-traffic pages, which should lift non-branded clicks.

Tie the headline to leads or revenue whenever you can. A reader who sees "traffic is up" learns nothing about whether the work pays.

### KPI Scorecard

Pick five to eight KPIs and keep them the same every month. For each, show this month, last month, the same month last year, a target, and a status. Year-over-year matters because search demand is seasonal, and a month-over-month dip in December says little. The spreadsheet's scorecard tab lists more candidate rows than you need, so clear the ones you will not stand behind every month.

### Organic Traffic and Conversions

Start from the Organic Search channel in Google Analytics. Report organic sessions, organic key events, and the key-event rate, calculated as key events divided by sessions. It counts repeat key events inside one session, so keep the definition the same every month. Which events count as key events is yours to decide.

### Search Console: Brand vs Non-Brand

**Report non-branded clicks on their own, because branded clicks come from people who already know you.** They rise with PR and word of mouth, so a blended number can look like SEO growth when search found nobody new.

Filter the Performance report's queries to exclude your brand terms, including misspellings and product names, and review that list every quarter. Tab 2 of the spreadsheet does the split for your top queries: type your brand terms once on the Start Here tab and it labels every query. It also marks non-branded queries with at least 50 impressions that sit at positions 4 to 5 or 11 to 15 as the next candidates for work, and those whose CTR is under half the expected CTR for their position. Both thresholds are settings on the tab. The expected-CTR column holds starting values, so replace them with your own site's curve, and treat a low-CTR flag as a prompt to check the title or an answer box.

Be careful with average position. Google [defines the position value](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7042828) as the topmost position your property held for each query, averaged across every query where it appeared.

So when a new batch of long-tail queries starts showing you on page two, the average gets worse while clicks hold or rise. Google adds that the figure differs from what any one searcher saw, because of location and search history. Report position by query group or page.

### Rankings, Winners and Losers

Track a fixed keyword set, one you chose on purpose and change rarely. Report how many sit in the top 3 and top 10, and name the biggest gainers and losers with a one-line reason each. Note any month a new SERP feature appeared on a tracked term.

A rank tracker checks chosen keywords from a location you set, while Search Console averages real impressions, so show them side by side and never swap one for the other. If the client asks how they compare with rivals, add a rank-overlap view against a few named competitors for the terms that matter commercially. Tab 4 of the spreadsheet holds the keyword set with its top 3 and top 10 counts.

### Layer 1: Platform Reports

Start with the free first-party data. Google's [generative AI performance report for Search](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) in Search Console covers AI Overviews and AI Mode, and Google says it reached all websites worldwide as of August 31, 2026. Google Discover has its own separate report.

The report groups data by page, country, device and date, and its data comes from the Web search type in the Performance report. The usual Performance report limits apply, including the 1,000-row limit on tables, so export the data each month and keep your own history. If you cannot see it, [Google says](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) the cause may be too few impressions in generative AI features, or that your site is excluded from them.

Google's documentation describes impressions only. Read the report as a visibility trend, not a traffic source, and note that queries are not among its listed dimensions. That is why it still does not replace an [AI Overview tracker](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/ai-overview-tracker).

Bing has a first-party report for Microsoft's side, though it counts citations rather than impressions. [AI Performance in Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/ai-performance-9f8e7d6c) covers Microsoft Copilot, AI-generated summaries in Bing and select partner integrations. It shows total citations, average cited pages per day, grounding queries and page-level counts, viewable for 7 days, 30 days, 3 months or a custom range.

Bing states that the data is a sample of overall citation activity, and that trends cannot be attributed to a specific cause. It also says the report does not measure rankings, authority or importance. Microsoft has since added preview views for Intents, Topics, [Citation Share and a period-over-period Compare view](https://blogs.bing.com/search/June-2026/New-AI-Visibility-Insights-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Intents-Topics-Citation-Share-Compare). Citation Share is your percentage of the citations shown for a grounding query, and Bing calls it an observational metric, not traffic share.

### Layer 2: GA4's AI Assistant Channel

GA4 groups sessions from sources like ChatGPT, Gemini, Deepseek, Copilot and Grok into an AI Assistant channel. [Google's channel definitions](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9756891) say a session lands in it when its medium is ai-assistant, which GA4 sets when the referrer matches Google's list of AI assistants, and that the channel excludes Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode.

Google introduces the list as sources like those named, so it is not exhaustive, and a visit that does not match lands in another channel. Treat the channel as a count of what GA4 can attribute, and note the date if Google's list changes. Perplexity is not among the sources Google names, so check where your own Perplexity visits land.

### Layer 3: A Fixed Prompt Panel

Neither layer above shows what ChatGPT or Perplexity say about you. For that you need a panel: a fixed list of the questions your buyers ask, run on the same engines on a schedule, with the answers logged. A list of about 20 is a workable start. Run each prompt more than once, because the answer changes between runs (see [how to measure your AI visibility](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/how-to-track-ai-visibility)).

The log on tab 5 of the spreadsheet holds up to 600 runs across months, and the sheet counts only rows with a 1 or 0 in all three outcome columns. Past a few dozen prompts, running and logging by hand stops being practical.

Then report these rates separately: **mention** (your brand is named), **citation** (your URL is shown as a source) and **recommendation** (you are named as a pick). They are different outcomes, and a brand can earn one without the others. Each rate is a share of your own logged runs, which is not market share.

Print the run count next to each rate, for example 7 of 10 runs, so the reader can see how little data sits under the percentage. The engine table on tab 5 shows this month's run count beside this month's rates, but tab 7 shows the citation rate alone, so carry the run count into your sentence.

Keep the panel stable: when you add or drop a prompt, write it in the change log and start a new trend line. If citations rise the month after a page update, you saw a correlation, not proof of cause.

## Reconcile Search Console Clicks and GA4 Sessions Before Your Client Does

Expect clicks and sessions to disagree, print the definitions on the report, and compare trends instead of totals. Google's guide on [using Search Console and Google Analytics together](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/google-analytics-search-console) says clicks and sessions are calculated differently, so you will likely see different numbers. A small gap is normal and needs no fix. A big one deserves a look.

Google lists what it calls the most common causes of a big gap:

1. Analytics tag problems, such as pages where the tag is missing
2. Users who opt out of tracking
3. Time zones: Search Console uses Pacific Time and cannot be changed, while GA4 lets you choose
4. Attribution: Search Console counts every click, while GA4 applies an attribution model
5. Canonical URLs: Search Console reports only the Google Search [canonical URL](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/canonical-tags)
6. Non-HTML files such as PDFs, which Search Console includes by default
7. Bots: GA4 excludes known bots, and Search Console does not necessarily filter them
8. Traffic breakdowns: Search Console splits traffic into web, image, video, news and Discover, and GA4 groups it differently

To compare like with like, run the comparison on GA4 sessions filtered to Session source = google and Session medium = organic, which is what Google's own dashboard template does. Keep the scorecard's sessions as the whole Organic Search channel, and footnote the scope: "Clicks: Search Console, Web search, Pacific Time. Sessions: GA4 Organic Search channel, all search engines."

## Explain a Bad Month Without Making Excuses

Lead with the drop: how big it is, where it happened, which kind of cause it is, and what you will do. A report that buries a bad month makes the next good one less believable.

Sort the cause before you write the sentence. Four classes make a first sort:

Then write it in a few plain lines. Written out:

> Organic clicks fell from 480 to 440. Impressions held, but click-through fell on the five pages where an AI answer now appears. We are rewriting those answers and adding the pages to the prompt panel.

Watch the cause. Google documents its generative AI report as impressions only, so it cannot show which clicks an AI answer took. Say "consistent with" when that is all you have, and annotate the chart with the date the change began.

## The One-Page Version and How Often to Send It

The executive version is five numbers, one risk and one decision. The numbers: organic sessions, organic key events, non-branded clicks, AI Assistant sessions, and your citation rate on the prompt panel. They come from the scorecard (section 2) and the AI section (9), so sections 3 to 8 stay in the full report. Under the numbers, one sentence on the biggest risk and one line on the decision you need from the reader. Tab 7 of the spreadsheet pulls the five numbers from the other tabs, so you write only the sentences.

If clients open the report mainly after a number has already dropped, agree two or three alert thresholds with them before the quarter starts and write them in the Target column of the scorecard. Name the window too, such as month to date against the same month last year, so a breach starts a conversation without waiting for the next report. A twelve-month chart beside the month also stops one weak month reading as a trend; the file has no such view, so build it in your dashboard.

Send the full report monthly. Organic results move over weeks, and a weekly report invites reactions to noise. Weekly updates make sense only around a migration or a launch, when something can break fast. Once a quarter, add a review of year-over-year trends, the tracked keyword set and the prompt panel. That cadence is a convention, so adjust it to how fast your client's decisions move.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should an SEO report include?

An executive summary, a KPI scorecard, organic traffic and conversions, brand and non-brand Search Console data, rankings, top landing pages, technical exceptions, backlinks, AI visibility and next steps.

### How long should an SEO report be?

One page for the executive version. The monthly report should run as long as it takes to answer each section's question, with raw tables in an appendix. If a section does not change a decision, cut it.

### What is an AI visibility report?

An AI visibility report shows how often a brand is mentioned, cited or recommended in AI-generated answers, plus the visits that arrive from AI assistants. It draws on platform reports, the GA4 AI Assistant channel and a fixed prompt panel, reported separately with the method stated.

### Can ChatGPT build an SEO report or do an SEO audit?

It can help you structure a report and draft the commentary from numbers you give it. It does not have your Search Console, GA4 or crawl data unless you export it or connect a data source, so a report or an audit still needs real data from your own tools, and every figure it writes has to be checked against the original. Keep client data within what your agreement allows.

## Where to Start

Fix your definitions first, then fill one month. In this order:

1. Download the report template and fill the inputs on the Start Here tab: the reporting month, your brand terms, the GA4 key events that count, and the date range
2. Fill tabs 1 to 4 from Search Console, GA4 and your rank tracker for last month, then enter the month before so the KPIs and their changes calculate
3. Open the generative AI performance report, check the GA4 AI Assistant channel, and log your first prompts on tab 5
4. Write the executive summary and the next-steps table last

If this is your first AI visibility report, run geotoolbox's free [AI Readiness checker](https://geotoolbox.ai/tools/ai-readiness) first: it tests robots.txt, AI-crawler access, content signals, sitemap and markdown in one pass, with no sign-up.
