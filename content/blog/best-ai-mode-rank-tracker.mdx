## What an AI Mode Rank Tracker Actually Does

An AI Mode rank tracker tells you whether [Google's AI Mode](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/what-is-google-ai-mode) names or cites your brand when someone asks a question in it, how often, and who it cites instead of you. That is a different job from classic rank tracking, and the word "rank" is misleading here. AI Mode does not return a numbered list. It reads a question, fans it out into sub-questions, and writes one synthesized answer with a handful of inline citations. There is no position three to hold.

So a good tracker measures four things, not a ranking:

- **Inclusion:** did your brand or domain appear in the answer at all, yes or no
- **Mention:** was your brand named in the answer text
- **Citation:** did AI Mode link one of your URLs as a source (only a linked citation can send a click)
- **Share of voice:** across a set of prompts, what fraction of the answer space is yours versus named competitors

Being named and being cited are not the same. AI Mode can describe your product without linking you, and only the link drives traffic. A brand can be named in 40% of answers for a topic and cited in 10% of them, until a page built around that exact question closes the gap. Tracking both is the point.

The harder truth sits underneath all of this: Google AI Mode has no public API for rank tracking. Every tool on the market samples it by driving a real browser session and reading what comes back. That makes each number a modeled estimate from repeated probes, not a meter reading of what real users saw. It is the same limit we describe in our guide to [tracking your brand across AI engines](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/ai-rank-tracker): you are measuring presence across many samples, not a fixed position, so the trend matters more than any single check. Keep that in mind while you read the rest of this comparison, because a vendor that hides it is selling you confidence it cannot deliver.

Most tracking mistakes start here, because several different surfaces get called the same thing. They are not interchangeable, and a tool that watches one is blind to the others.

Classic search is the familiar list of links, where you either rank on page one or you do not. An [AI Overview](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/what-are-google-ai-overviews) is the summary box stitched onto the top of a normal results page, and it only appears for a share of queries. AI Mode is a separate, full-page conversational experience, reached by opting into the AI Mode tab (its own `udm=50` view), where a search becomes a back-and-forth and one question fans out into many. It is not a fringe surface: Google said at I/O 2026 that AI Mode had [passed one billion monthly users](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/) within a year of launch.

Why treat them separately? Because winning one does not win the others. Ahrefs, comparing both surfaces across hundreds of thousands of queries, found the [cited sources overlap just 13.7%](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-vs-ai-mode/) of the time for the same query. A brand that owns the AI Overview can be absent from the AI Mode answer to the same starting question. AI Mode answers also run longer and fan out further, so they pull from a different mix of pages, a pattern we cover in [query fan-out](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/query-fan-out) and in [Google AI Mode SEO](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/google-ai-mode-seo). That mix leans heavily on Google itself: SE Ranking's analysis of 1.3M AI Mode citations found [Google's own domains are the single most-cited source at 17.42%](https://seranking.com/blog/google-links-in-ai-mode-answers/), so the winnable ground is usually your buyer's specific question, not the head term Google already owns.

Google's own tooling reinforces the split. In June 2026 it began rolling out, in stages, a [generative-AI performance report](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports) in Search Console, but it reports impressions for AI Overviews and AI Mode counted together, with no click data and no way to isolate AI Mode. So even Google will not hand you a clean AI Mode number. That gap is exactly what a dedicated tracker exists to fill.

## How AI Mode Trackers Actually Work

Knowing the mechanics tells you what you are paying for, and which vendor claims to distrust. Because there is no public API, the tools work in broadly the same way: each drives a real browser session, runs your prompts against AI Mode on a schedule, and parses what comes back.

The pipeline has a few steps:

1. **Execute:** run each prompt in a headless browser, usually through residential proxies, with a set country, device, and logged-out state so runs are comparable
2. **Capture:** store the full answer text, inline citations, and follow-up suggestions
3. **Detect and extract:** use language processing to find brand mentions and pull the cited URLs
4. **Score:** classify sentiment around each mention and aggregate inclusion, citation rate, and share of voice over time

Two things follow from this. First, the quality of a tool's proxy and parsing infrastructure decides how reliable its data is, which is why two trackers can report different numbers for the same prompt. Second, and more important, none of them see real user prompts. Every tool samples controlled questions on a schedule and infers your standing. Treat the output as a modeled estimate, not ground truth.

That matters because AI Mode is volatile. The same prompt can return different citations by time, location, and login state, and Google ships model updates mid-week that shift the answer overnight. A single check is close to meaningless. A tracker earns its price by running a fixed prompt set repeatedly and showing you the trend, not by telling you where you sat at 3pm on a Tuesday.

One buyer-beware note on the marketing. Because there is no public feed, be skeptical of any vendor that claims its data comes "direct from Google" or from a private pipeline. These tools generally scrape the same logged-out interface you could open yourself. The differences are in coverage, cadence, and how cleanly they parse the answer, not in privileged access nobody actually has.

## What to Look For When Choosing

Before the tool list, here is the short checklist that separates a real AI Mode tracker from a dashboard that bolts the label on.

**Is Google AI Mode in the plan you can actually afford?** This is the trap. Several tools advertise a low entry price, then put AI Mode behind a higher tier or a paid add-on. Profound's entry plan tracks ChatGPT only. Otterly bills AI Mode as an add-on on top of its base price. Scrunch keeps AI Mode for its Enterprise tier. The list price and the AI-Mode price are often different numbers, which is why the comparison table below is anchored on the cheapest tier that actually includes it.

**Does it track citations at the URL level, not just mentions?** A mention tells you AI Mode said your name. A URL-level citation tells you which of your pages it linked, which is the thing you can actually replicate. Domain-only aggregates hide that.

**How many engines beyond AI Mode?** Most brands also want ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini. Google AI Mode is one surface, and buying a single-engine tool usually means buying several. Coverage breadth is where budgets get eaten, so match it to where your buyers actually search.

**Refresh cadence, and how many times each prompt runs.** Daily catches fast movement during launches; weekly is fine for stable content. Anything monthly, which is where some SEO-suite add-ons land, is a blind spot in a surface that can shift within a week. Ask how many times a tool re-runs each prompt per cycle too, because a single sample of a nondeterministic answer is noise, and averaging several is the whole point.

**Does it follow the conversation, not just the first question?** AI Mode is multi-turn by design. Ask "best email software" and you might be cited first, but the follow-up "which has the better mobile app?" can hand the answer to a competitor. Tools that only capture the opening query miss half the story. The better ones run follow-up chains and show whether you hold the citation as the dialogue deepens.

**Geo and market coverage, exports, and an API.** If you sell in more than one country, AI Mode citations differ by market, and you need per-country tracking plus raw exports to do anything serious with the data.

**The reachability check most tools skip.** A tracker tells you that you are missing. It rarely tells you why. Often the reason is upstream: the page an AI answer should cite is blocked, slow, or renders nothing without JavaScript, so it never becomes an eligible source. Before paying for any tracker, confirm the AI crawlers can even load your pages, because a tracker cannot fix a page it can only keep reporting as absent.

## How to Confirm a Tool Really Tracks AI Mode (Not AI Overviews)

Vendor demos show polished charts. Few prove the charts come from AI Mode rather than the AI Overview block or a generic Gemini API call. You can settle it in about thirty minutes with seven checks before you sign anything.

1. **Ask for a timestamped screenshot of the actual AI Mode tab.** Not parsed data, not the AI Overview box. The real AI Mode interface, with the full answer and its inline citations. If a vendor cannot show it, they are probably tracking Overviews and calling it AI Mode.
2. **Check citations are captured at the URL level.** The dashboard should show specific cited pages, for example `competitor.com/pricing` as its own line, not just "competitor.com cited five times."
3. **Confirm queries run logged-out and per-country.** Configure the same prompt for two countries and verify the citations differ. Identical results across countries, or a logged-in session, means personalization is contaminating the data.
4. **Make them separate AI Mode from AI Overviews.** A credible vendor can explain the technical difference and show distinct data for each surface. Interchangeable language is a red flag.
5. **Get raw answer-text export, not just metrics.** You want the full captured answer with timestamps in a CSV or API, so you can audit what the tool actually saw.
6. **Verify it queries live, localized AI Mode.** Some tools approximate AI Mode by calling a Gemini API, which is not the same product and cites a different mix of sources. Ask directly whether the data comes from the live AI Mode interface in your target markets.
7. **Check it handles follow-up prompts.** AI Mode is a conversation, so ask whether the tool can track a follow-up question and show how the citations shift between the opening query and the next one. Single-query-only tools miss where you lose the thread.

A tool that passes all seven is doing the real job. One that stumbles on the screenshot or the AI-Mode-versus-Overviews question is selling you a relabeled Overview tracker, and you should price it accordingly.

## Free and DIY: Can You Track AI Mode Without Paying?

Partly, and it is worth doing before you buy anything. The manual method costs nothing but time.

Pick the ten questions where being cited in AI Mode would matter most to your business. Open the AI Mode tab in a clean, logged-out session, ask each one, and log whether you are cited, who is cited instead, and which of your URLs appears. Re-run the set on a schedule, weekly is enough for most brands, and watch the trend rather than any single result. Pair it with two free signals: the Search Console generative-AI report, which shows AI Overview and AI Mode impressions (lumped together, no clicks), and your branded-search trend, which tends to lift when AI answers start naming you. Our walkthrough on [how to measure your AI visibility](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/how-to-track-ai-visibility) covers the manual setup in more depth, and the [AI visibility audit](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/ai-visibility-audit) turns it into a repeatable checklist.

What free will not give you is scale or memory. Ten prompts by hand is manageable; a hundred prompts across three countries, re-run weekly with stored history and competitor share of voice, is not. Search Console will not split AI Mode from Overviews or show you a single click, and it cannot tell you which competitor took the citation you wanted. The moment you need more than a spot check, a repeatable prompt set, or a number you can put in front of a client, the manual method stops paying for itself. That is the line where a paid tracker earns its price, and the tools compared above are how you cross it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between an AI Mode tracker, an AI Overviews tracker, and a classic rank tracker?

A classic rank tracker reports your position in the list of blue links. An AI Overviews tracker watches the AI summary box that appears above those links on select queries. An AI Mode tracker watches Google's separate conversational tab, where there is no ranked list, only a synthesized answer with citations. They are different surfaces with different data, and Ahrefs found the sources cited in AI Overviews and AI Mode overlap only 13.7% of the time, so one tool cannot stand in for another.

### Can you reliably track Google AI Mode when it is nondeterministic and personalized with no public API?

You can track the trend, not an exact rank. Every tool samples AI Mode by running prompts through a browser on a schedule, so the same prompt can return different answers depending on when and where it runs. A single check is unreliable, but running a fixed prompt set repeatedly and reading the direction of travel is meaningful. Treat any tool's number as an informed approximation, and be wary of vendors that present it as exact.

### Can I track Google AI Mode for free with Search Console?

Only partly. Since June 2026, Search Console has been rolling out a generative-AI performance report, but it counts AI Overview and AI Mode impressions together, shows no clicks, and gives you no way to isolate AI Mode. For a free spot check, ask your top buyer questions in the AI Mode tab yourself and log whether you are cited. For prompt-level coverage, competitor citations, and history, you need a dedicated tool.

### How much does an AI Mode rank tracker cost, and why is real coverage gated above the entry tier?

Plans that include AI Mode start around €20 a month at the budget end and run into custom enterprise pricing. The trap is that several tools advertise a low headline price, then place AI Mode behind an add-on or a higher tier: Profound and Scrunch reserve it for Enterprise, Otterly and SE Ranking charge an add-on on top of the base plan, and geotoolbox (ours) puts it a tier above its own entry plan. Always confirm which plan actually includes AI Mode before comparing prices.

### Does Google AI Mode actually drive traffic?

Less than classic search, and it is harder to measure. AI Mode answers are designed to satisfy the question in place, so they generate fewer clicks than a page of links, and briefly at launch in May 2025 the links even carried a [noreferrer attribute](https://searchengineland.com/googles-ai-mode-traffic-untrackable-455883) that hid the traffic from analytics until Google fixed it within days. The value today is less about direct clicks and more about being the source Google's AI trusts, which supports branded search and downstream demand. Measure presence and share of voice, and watch branded search for lift.

### Which AI Mode rank tracker is best?

There is no single winner, because the right tool depends on your job. Dedicated platforms like LLM Pulse go deepest on AI visibility, suite add-ons like SE Ranking and Semrush suit teams consolidating tools, and enterprises with compliance needs pay a premium for Scrunch or Profound. Pick on whether AI Mode is in a plan you can afford, how many other engines you need, and whether you want the fix or just the flag.
