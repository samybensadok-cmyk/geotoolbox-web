A homeowner with a leaking roof or a dead AC unit calls whoever they find first: in the map pack (the three map results), the results below it or an AI answer. Contractor SEO decides whether that's you.

In our test of four AI assistants, asked about contractors in Dallas, Phoenix and Atlanta, AI Mode cited Google's listings and ChatGPT mostly cited best-of lists.

## What Contractor SEO Is and What It Replaces

Contractor SEO is the work that gets a roofer, plumber or general contractor found by homeowners in the Google map pack, the regular results, AI Overviews and AI assistants such as ChatGPT.

Our [local SEO guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/local-seo) explains how each of those ranks businesses; here is what local SEO for contractors adds.

Without SEO or steady referrals, you pay per lead. Angi and Thumbtack send calls right away, but a common complaint in the five Reddit threads about them we read was paying for leads that other companies received too. Google's [Local Services Ads](https://business.google.com/us/ad-solutions/local-service-ads/) also charge per lead.

SEO leads carry no per-lead fee, but the work takes months, and in a crowded metro a profile far from the searcher may never reach the map pack. Paid leads can carry you meanwhile: run Local Services Ads or Google Ads on your best-paying jobs, then cut back as calls from search grow.

## What AI Assistants Cite When Homeowners Ask for a Contractor

Google AI Mode cited Google's business listings most, ChatGPT best-of lists, and Gemini contractors' own websites.

We tested this at geotoolbox on November 15, 2026, with 15 prompts: emergency AC repair, an emergency plumber, a licensed electrician for a panel upgrade, storm-damage roof repair and a kitchen remodeler, each in Dallas, Phoenix and Atlanta. A typical prompt read "Who is the best emergency plumber in Phoenix, Arizona? Recommend a few and explain why you picked them. Cite sources."

Each prompt ran twice, minutes apart, in ChatGPT (logged out, web search on), Gemini (logged out), Google AI Mode and Perplexity's API, through DataForSEO with the location set to the US, not the city. That gave 120 answers and **423 unique sources**, counting each page once per prompt and engine. We counted a page only when the engine cited it, not every page it retrieved.

## Set Up Your Google Business Profile as a Service-Area Business

If customers don't come to your address, Google treats you as a service-area business.

### Profile Rules for Home-Based Trades

- **Hide the address.** Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) give the example of a plumber who runs the business from home: clear the address and show service areas instead. Expect to show mostly near your base: Google's ranking page counts distance from the searcher.
- **Keep the area realistic.** The guidelines say it usually "shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time" from your base, though some businesses can justify more. A second profile is allowed only for a location with its own staff and its own service area.
- **Use the real name.** "Including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted" and can get the profile suspended, so "Smith Roofing" stays "Smith Roofing", not "Smith Roofing Dallas Hail Repair".
- **Pick the trade as the primary category.** Google asks for "the fewest number of categories" that describe the core business, so add secondary categories only for services you actually sell.
- **Never list a virtual office.** A rented mailing address you don't work from isn't eligible, and neither is a P.O. box.
- **Keep it complete and current.** Google's [ranking help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) says to "provide complete and detailed business info." Add photos of finished jobs, update hours and services when they change, and post when a new season starts.

### Verification and Fake Competitors

Verification and suspensions came up in four of the contractor and Local Search Forum threads we read. The local SEO guide linked above lists the triggers and the evidence to keep ready.

To report a fake or keyword-stuffed competitor, suggest an edit on its listing in Google Maps first. If the name, phone number or website itself is fraudulent, Google's [Business Redressal Complaint Form](https://support.google.com/business/contact/business_redressal_form?hl=en) takes complaints about "misleading information or fraudulent activity" on those three fields. Explain the problem in detail and keep screenshots.

### City Pages Without Doorway Spam

City pages are where contractors get into trouble. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) list "pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse. A page for a town where you've done dozens of jobs, with photos, the permits it requires and reviews from customers there, has something of its own to say, as long as it isn't a near-copy of your other city pages.

### Fast, Phone-Friendly Pages

Put the call button at the top of every page and keep the estimate form to a few fields. Google "highly recommend[s] site owners achieve good Core Web Vitals for success with Search" ([Core Web Vitals guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals)); those are its scores for how fast and stable a page loads. Open the Core Web Vitals report in Search Console, Google's free site dashboard, before you pay for a redesign. Our [technical SEO guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/technical-seo) covers the fixes; oversized project photos are a common culprit.

### Guides That Answer Homeowners' Questions

Service pages catch people ready to call. Guides catch them a few weeks earlier: what a roof replacement costs and why, repair or replace, which permits a panel upgrade needs. Use price ranges from your own invoices rather than national averages, and write for the homeowner reading it, which is what Google's [people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) guidance asks for: content "created primarily for people."

### Schema Markup for Your Trade

Schema markup is code on your pages that tells search engines what kind of business you are. Schema.org's [HomeAndConstructionBusiness](https://schema.org/HomeAndConstructionBusiness) type has subtypes for RoofingContractor, HVACBusiness, Plumber, Electrician and GeneralContractor; use the one that fits, with the same name and phone number as your profile, and if you've hidden your street address on Google, give only city, state and ZIP. Our guide to [schema markup for AI](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/schema-markup-for-ai) covers testing it.

## Reviews, Licenses and the Listings That Vouch for You

### Asking for Reviews within the Rules

Ask every customer for a Google review when the job is done, by text, with your review link. Asking everyone is allowed; paying for reviews or asking only the happy ones isn't.

Google's [Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) bans incentives "in exchange for posting any review" and bans businesses that "selectively solicit positive reviews from customers". It also bars asking for specific content, such as naming the technician, and setting staff quotas for reviews, so skip tech bonuses tied to named reviews.

The FTC's [rule on consumer reviews](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-22/html/2024-18519.htm), in force since October 21, 2024, bars incentives conditioned on a review's sentiment and fake reviews. Google's rule is stricter, so a discount for "a review" still breaks it.

In [BrightLocal's survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), 74% of consumers only care about reviews written in the last three months. Reply to all your Google reviews, the bad ones included. And the contractor with the most reviews doesn't always win the map pack: Google's [ranking help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) weighs relevance and distance alongside reviews and links.

### Credentials AI Answers Mention

Make your credentials easy to verify. In our test, the AI answers named licensing and manufacturer certifications far more often than they linked to them, so:

- **Show your license number.** If your state licenses your trade, put the number on your website and check it matches the state's lookup (the [Texas TDLR search](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/LicenseSearch/), for example).
- **Claim your listings.** Set up BBB, Angi, Houzz, Yelp, Apple Business Connect and Bing Places with the same name, phone number and service area.
- **Show manufacturer certifications** (GAF, Carrier) and ask the maker to link your dealer-locator entry to your site, an easy local link.

### When to Publish Seasonal Pages

New pages take time to rank, so update service pages before searches start climbing: AC repair by February, furnace repair by September, storm and hail pages by February. Emergency trades should also keep their profile hours exact: 47 of the 48 emergency HVAC and plumbing answers in our test mentioned 24/7 service (the prompts asked for emergency help). List it only if a person really answers at night.

## Measure Calls and Booked Jobs, Not Rankings

Count calls, form requests and booked jobs, and tie each one back to the page or listing that produced it.

Three pieces cover most contractors:

1. **Calls.** Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) require a number "under the direct control of the business" that doesn't redirect callers elsewhere. A common practice, not a Google rule: a call-tracking number that rings your own line goes in the primary slot and your main number in the additional-phone field. Own that tracking number yourself, not through the agency.
2. **Forms and website visits.** Mark estimate-form leads as key events (Google Analytics' name for conversions), and add UTM tags, a short tracking code at the end of a link, to your profile's website link so those visits show up as their own source.
3. **Jobs.** Add a "how did you find us" field to your customer database (CRM) or job sheet and fill it for every booked estimate. Judge an agency on that number.

Check AI answers monthly too: the same prompts ("emergency plumber in [your city]"), the same engines, more than one run each, since answers can change from one run to the next. ## What Contractor SEO Costs and How to Hire Help

Most of the work is within reach of an owner: the profile, a review habit, service pages and job photos. Our [small business SEO guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/seo-for-small-business) covers the free first-month setup. Bring in help for a site with technical problems, several locations or a crowded metro.

Prices for contractor SEO services from agencies and digital marketing firms vary too much for a single figure. One widely cited survey, [Ahrefs' poll of 439 SEO providers](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing/) (last updated in August 2024), put local SEO at $1,557 a month on average, across all industries.

### Four Checks Before You Sign

1. **No guarantees.** Google's [guide to hiring an SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo) says "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google" and warns about anyone claiming a "special relationship" with Google.
2. **You own the assets.** Your domain, website, Business Profile and analytics logins stay in your name.
3. **Reports show calls and jobs.** Rankings alone don't count.
4. **An exit you can live with.** Check the minimum term and what happens to your site if you leave.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does contractor SEO take?

Profile and review fixes usually pay off before new service pages, which can take months in a competitive city. No one can promise a date; Google's hiring guide suggests asking any SEO "What kind of results do you expect to see, and in what timeframe?"

### Is SEO worth it for a small contracting business?

Usually, yes, if you want more work than referrals bring. The profile, a review habit and a few service pages cost mostly time. It's less urgent if referrals already keep your crews booked.

### Can ChatGPT or my website builder do my SEO?

They can draft pages and titles, but they can't verify your profile, collect reviews or photograph your jobs. Add your real jobs, photos and prices to anything AI drafts.
