## AEO Tools Are Mostly GEO Tools

Before spending anything, know what you are buying. Answer engine optimization (AEO) and generative engine optimization (GEO) describe almost the same work, and the tools reflect that. G2 files answer engine optimization software under a category it also labels generative engine optimization, and the same vendors appear on "best AEO tools" and "best GEO tools" lists alike.

Where a real line exists, it is not AEO versus GEO. It is between tools that only measure and tools that also change something. Most of this category measures: it reports whether you are named and cited. A smaller set adds remediation, generating schema, rewriting pages, or flagging what to fix. That divide matters more for your budget than the acronym on the box. Our explainer on [GEO versus AEO versus SEO](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/geo-vs-aeo-vs-seo) draws the full comparison.

There is also a fair argument that none of this is new, that AEO is SEO with a fresh acronym. The kernel is right: clear, trustworthy, well-structured content carries straight over, and our [AEO best practices](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/aeo-best-practices) checklist is those fundamentals as a page-by-page audit. What is genuinely different is the unit of measurement. Traditional SEO tracks rankings and clicks. An AEO tool tracks whether your brand is named and cited inside a generated answer across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI Overviews.

That shift is the whole reason the category exists. Being named inside the answer is increasingly the only visibility there is: Pew Research found people click a result on just [8% of searches that show an AI summary](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-hurting-clicks-study-459434), against 15% without one. And AI Overviews now appear on [nearly half of Google searches](https://www.brightedge.com/resources/weekly-ai-search-insights/ai-overviews-one-year-presence-size-citing) by BrightEdge's tracking. Most of the tools below measure that visibility. They just do it for different budgets and jobs.

## What an AEO Tool Actually Does

Strip away the marketing and an AEO tool does three jobs, plus a prerequisite most ignore.

1. **Track mentions and citations.** It runs a fixed set of prompts on a schedule and records whether the engines name you, whether they cite you as a source, and your share of voice against competitors.
2. **Benchmark competitors.** It shows the same data for the rivals that surface in your answers, so you can see the gap.
3. **Tell you what to change.** The better tools turn that data into specific moves: schema to add, pages to restructure, content to write.

That first job hides a distinction worth insisting on. A **mention** is your brand named in an answer with no link. A **citation** is your page linked as a source in that answer. They are not the same signal, and a tool that reports one blended "visibility score" can hide which one you are missing. A low score might mean you are never named, named but never cited, or, worst of all, that the engine never read your site.

That last case is the prerequisite few trackers check: reachability. A tool can report a confident zero share of voice when the real problem is that the AI crawler could not fetch or render your pages. Keep it in mind, because it is the cheapest thing to fix and the easiest to miss.

When you compare tools, five things separate a platform from a dashboard: how many engines it covers, whether it tracks citations or only mentions, whether it works at the individual-prompt level, how often it refreshes, and whether it tells you what to do next. Price matters too, and it varies more than most buyers assume.

[**Peec AI**](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/what-is-peec-ai) sits a step up at €85 a month (€70 billed annually), and is the tool practitioners most often name when they want depth without enterprise pricing. **AIclicks** (around $59 a month) and **Rank Prompt** (around $49) round out the budget tier, the latter built for agencies juggling several clients.

It tracks the same mentions and citations as the tools above, and leads with the reachability layer covered below, a free Agent Readiness scan and a [Content Analyzer](https://geotoolbox.ai/features/content-analyzer) that check whether the engines can fetch, render, and cite your pages. ### If You Need Deeper Citation and Content Intelligence

Larger teams want more than a mention count: prompt-volume data, citation-source analysis, and remediation. This is the expensive end.

**Profound** is the category benchmark, tracking citations and sentiment across the top answer engines using front-end data rather than API calls alone, which matters because what an API returns can differ from what a real user sees. Its Starter plan is $99 a month but covers ChatGPT only; Growth at $399 a month opens three engines. The money agrees with the benchmark label: Profound [closed a $96 million Series C at a $1 billion valuation](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/profound-raises-96m-series-c) in February 2026. **Scrunch AI** and **AthenaHQ** focus on citation patterns and auditing, with [Scrunch's entry plan](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/scrunch-ai-review) at $250 a month across four engines and adding LLM crawler analytics, and AthenaHQ self-serve from $295 a month ($245 annual) with a free Essential tier, the widest entry engine coverage at ten, and blind-spot detection. Scrunch [became a Sitecore company](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sitecore-acquires-scrunch-to-help-brands-influence-discovery-and-buying-decisions-in-the-ai-search-era-302790214.html) in June 2026. **Conductor** and **Evertune** target enterprises, the latter at $800 a month for its Pro tier and custom pricing above it for large-scale prompt testing built to reduce the run-to-run noise that makes single checks unreliable. These are powerful and priced accordingly; only the citation depth and compliance needs of a large brand justify the cost.

If you run an SEO platform, the cheapest AEO tool may be one you already have. **Ahrefs** offers a free one-shot AI visibility checker for a snapshot, with its Brand Radar tracking on a paid plan from about $50 a month. **Semrush's AI Toolkit** adds visibility tracking as a [$99-a-month add-on](https://www.semrush.com/kb/1493-ai-visibility-toolkit) per domain, covering four engines, on top of a paid Semrush plan. **SE Ranking** layers an AI Search add-on at €79 a month (€63.20 billed annually), and **Surfer** adds AI tracking to its content tools. One development to factor in: Adobe [completed its acquisition of Semrush](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/04/adobe-completes-semrush-acquisition) on April 28, 2026, so expect the AI Toolkit's packaging to shift over time. None match a dedicated platform's prompt-volume depth, but putting AI metrics next to your keyword data is sensible and avoids paying twice. ### If You Want to Optimize Content to Be Cited

Tracking tells you where you stand; it does not write the page. A separate group leans toward the content side. **Writesonic** and **AirOps** pair generation with AEO workflows, and **Conductor** and **AthenaHQ** include writing assistants tuned for citations. Treat AI-generated drafts with care, though, because a model writing commodity content produces exactly what other models have no reason to cite. The [research that defined GEO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) found that citing sources, adding quotations, and including statistics are the changes that lift visibility. The tool can structure and check; the original substance has to be yours. The craft of writing a page that gets quoted is its own skill, which our guide to [AI content optimization](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/ai-content-optimization) covers at the sentence level, and for the tools that score that work, see our roundup of the [best content optimization tools](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/best-content-optimization-tools).

### The Prerequisite Most Trackers Skip

Every tool above assumes the answer engines can already read your pages. Often they cannot. [Google's own guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) is explicit that its generative features draw on crawlable content, so a page a bot cannot fetch is far less likely to be cited, however good it is. A firewall rule or a JavaScript-only render can turn an AI crawler away while every human sees the page fine, and a citation tracker will report that as a content problem when it is really an access one.

geotoolbox's free [Agent Readiness scan](https://geotoolbox.ai/tools/ai-readiness) checks that prerequisite: it fetches and renders your pages the way the AI crawlers do and flags what blocks them. You do not need our tool to look, though: check your robots.txt for rules that block AI crawlers like GPTBot and Google-Extended, watch your server logs for whether bots such as GPTBot and PerplexityBot actually reach you, and run a key page through Google's URL Inspection. Scrunch, for one, now surfaces some of this crawler-access data, but most trackers treat it as an afterthought. Across the sites we scan for AI visibility, a blocked or unrendered page is a common and under-diagnosed reason a brand shows zero AEO visibility despite ranking well in Google, so rule it out before you pay a tracker to confirm the same zero.

## The $0 Starting Point

You can answer the first question, "do the engines name me at all," without spending anything. Pick ten prompts a buyer would actually type, run each by hand in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews two or three times, since the answers shift from run to run, and record whether you are named, whether you are cited, and who shows up instead. A spreadsheet holds a month of that easily, and it tells you whether you have a visibility problem worth paying to track.

Pair it with the free reachability check first, since a blocked page makes every other number meaningless. geotoolbox's free tools, Ahrefs' one-shot checker, and the trials most budget trackers run will all establish a baseline for nothing. Our [best free SEO tools](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/best-free-seo-tools) guide covers the free option for each job.

Many small teams do not need a paid AEO tool yet. What a subscription buys is scale and history: dozens of prompts refreshed on a schedule, competitor benchmarking, and a record of whether your changes moved anything. Reach for it once the manual approach stops fitting in a spreadsheet.

## How to Choose Your AEO Tool

Match the tool to your situation, and weigh three things above the feature list: engine coverage, refresh cadence, and price.

**Engine coverage** comes first. Find out where your buyers actually ask their questions. If they live in ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews, you do not need a tool that charges extra for six engines you will never read, and you do want to confirm those two are in the entry tier rather than an upsell.

**Cadence** is the one people miss. Many tools are built to produce a monthly agency report, not to support daily iteration. If you need to test and adjust prompts often, confirm the refresh speed before you buy, not after. If a monthly snapshot is all you will look at, do not pay for daily.

**Price**, finally, with the reminder that the expensive tools are not automatically better for a small team. Once you only need the AEO slice, a full enterprise suite is usually poor value. So the practical path for most teams is simple: start with a free tier or a budget tracker, and upgrade only when you hit a real wall.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are AEO tools?

AEO tools measure and improve how answer engines represent your brand. Operationally, they replace a keyword list with a prompt set: instead of tracking positions for "best crm," you track whether ChatGPT names you when someone asks "what CRM should a 10-person agency use." The better ones also benchmark competitors in those answers and recommend changes.

### What is the best AEO tool?

There is no single best one, which is why any list that crowns its own product should be read with care. Choose by the job: HubSpot AEO or Otterly for cheap mention tracking, Peec AI for affordable depth, geotoolbox if you also want the reachability check, Profound or AthenaHQ for enterprise citation research, and a suite add-on like Semrush or Ahrefs Brand Radar if you already pay for SEO software. Confirm the engines and billing basis on the plan you would actually buy.

### Is AEO better than SEO?

It is not better, it is a different measurement layer on the same work. The content fundamentals carry over; what changes is what you track, citation share inside AI answers instead of rank and clicks, and per-engine reports instead of one Google view. Most teams add AEO tracking to an existing SEO program rather than replacing it.

### Is there a free AEO tool?

Yes, for limited use. AthenaHQ has a free Essential tier, geotoolbox's free tools check crawler access and AI readiness, Ahrefs offers a free one-shot snapshot, and most budget trackers run trials. The catch is that the free routes are one-off snapshots or capped plans, not ongoing tracking at volume, so they establish where you stand rather than watch it over time.

### Do AEO and GEO tools differ?

Mostly no. The terms are used interchangeably, and the same vendors appear on "best AEO tools" and "best GEO tools" lists alike. The line that does matter is between tools that only measure your visibility and tools that also help you change it. For buying purposes, treat AEO and GEO as the same category and sort by that measure-versus-optimize divide instead.

### What is the best AEO tool for a small business?

Decide by who reads the data. A solo marketer checking monthly does fine with HubSpot AEO, Otterly, or geotoolbox's Starter tier; a small team that wants shared dashboards gets more from Peec AI or a multi-seat plan once several engines and shared access start to matter. The enterprise platforms only pay off with compliance or prompt-volume needs a small business rarely has.

## Before You Pay, Check You Can Be Cited

The cheapest move in AEO is the one most trackers will not prompt you to make: confirm the answer engines can actually read your pages. A blocked crawler or a JavaScript-only render produces the same zero in a citation report as weak content, and only one of those is fixed by buying a tool. Run a free reachability check, like geotoolbox's [Agent Readiness scan](https://geotoolbox.ai/tools/ai-readiness), fix what it flags, then pick a tracker for the job you have. The best AEO tool is worth nothing on a page the engines cannot see.
