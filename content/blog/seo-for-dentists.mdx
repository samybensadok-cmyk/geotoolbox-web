Most advice on SEO for dentists stops at Google. Patients now also ask AI assistants which dentist to see, so we checked what those answers show.

For this guide, geotoolbox asked ChatGPT and Google AI Mode for the best dentists, implant dentists, emergency dentists, pediatric dentists and Invisalign dentists in three US cities, 60 answers in all. 107 of AI Mode's 132 references were Google Business Profile listings. ChatGPT's source links went mostly to best-of lists and health directories; two went to a practice's own site. Its practice cards did link practice websites.

So in our test, AI answers cited the profile and the directory listings far more often than the practice website. In our [contractor SEO test](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/seo-for-contractors), Google AI Mode leaned on listings for trades too: 59 of its 84 sources were Google business listings.

## What SEO for Dentists Covers in 2026

**SEO for dentists** is the work of getting a dental practice shown when nearby patients search for a dentist or a treatment, and turning those searches into booked appointments.

Patients meet your practice in three places in local search: the **map pack**, which reads your Business Profile, reviews and distance; the **organic results**, which read your website page by page; and **AI answers** from ChatGPT and Google AI Mode, which build a short list from whatever sources each engine trusts.

The mechanics of the first two are the same as for any local business, and our [local SEO guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/local-seo) covers them. Three things differ for a practice:

- Dental pages are health content, so Google holds them to a higher bar for expertise and accuracy
- HIPAA reaches into your review replies and your website tracking tags
- Patients search by treatment (implants, Invisalign, an emergency), so each treatment needs its own page

## What AI Assistants Show When Patients Ask for a Dentist

In our test, AI answers rarely cited a practice's own website as a source.

### How We Ran the Test

On November 15, 2026, we asked "What are the best [service] in [city]?" for dentists, dentists for dental implants, emergency dentists, pediatric dentists and Invisalign dentists in Phoenix, Charlotte and Minneapolis: 15 prompts, each run twice in ChatGPT and twice in Google AI Mode, 60 answers.

The plain "dentists" prompt, run in the same cities on the same day, is the control. With six answers per service and engine, read the differences as patterns, not rates.

ChatGPT ran logged out with web search on, from a US connection, with the city in the prompt; AI Mode ran with its location set to each city. We collected the answers through DataForSEO, counted every source link an answer showed, and sorted sources into types after reading them, using category definitions written before the run (a state dental board type was added afterwards). Every prompt asked for the "best" dentists, which favors best-of lists, and the two runs of a prompt often differed: ChatGPT showed 39 links in one round and 58 in the other.

### AI Mode Built Its Answers from Business Profiles

AI Mode showed 132 references across its 30 answers, and 107 of them were Google place listings, the same Business Profile data that feeds Google Maps.

The other 25 were 11 online booking tools such as Flexbook and NexHealth, 6 directory and referral links (3 of them Zocdoc profiles from one answer), 4 practice websites, 3 review pages and 1 ClearChoice consultation page. Seven carry tracking tags that name Google as the source, which suggests AI Mode took them from the listing rather than from the practice's site. 34 of its 123 practice cards also carried a website link; we counted cards separately from references.

## Set Up a Google Business Profile for a Dental Practice

Your Business Profile is what AI Mode cited most in our test, and it feeds the map pack. Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Distance is out of your hands; the four settings below are not.

### Choose Your Categories

Across the 300 Google Maps results we pulled for the test (217 distinct listings, some ranking in several searches), 148 results used **Dentist** as the primary category, 47 used **Pediatric dentist** and 30 used **Emergency dental service**. As secondary categories, 124 results listed Cosmetic dentist and 95 Emergency dental service. Google's guidelines ask you to choose the most specific category that represents your core business and to use as few categories as possible, so add secondary ones only for core services, such as emergency care you staff for.

Keep the profile name to your real-world practice name: Google's guidelines say details such as the city or services belong in the other fields, not in the name.

### Add Services and a Booking Link

List each treatment as a service in the profile, with a short description, and set the booking link. AI Mode showed booking pages directly in our test, including Flexbook and NexHealth pages and Zocdoc profiles, so a working booking link may be one route from an AI answer to your calendar.

### Keep Hours Accurate

If you take emergencies, show the hours you really answer the phone. In our test, AI Mode spelled out opening times only in its emergency answers, and its practice cards showed an Open or Closed status on every service, so wrong hours send patients elsewhere.

### Give Dentists Their Own Profiles

Google's [guidelines for individual practitioners](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) allow a dentist their own profile if they are public-facing and can be contacted directly at the verified location during stated hours. With several such dentists, each profile carries only the dentist's name and the practice keeps its own. When a dentist is the only public-facing practitioner of a branded office, Google says it's best to share one profile, named with the brand and then the dentist.

## Reviews: Get More Without Breaking HIPAA or Google's Rules

Reviews feed the map pack, the AI Mode cards and the directory profiles ChatGPT linked. Three sets of rules limit how you ask for them and how you reply.

### Google's Review Rules

Google's [Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) bars incentives for reviews, discouraging negative reviews and asking only happy patients. It also bars pressure on the premises, review quotas for staff and asking patients to mention a staff member by name. Ask every patient the same way, after the visit, with your review link. The [ADA](https://www.ada.org/en/resources/practice/legal-and-regulatory/managing-dental-practice-online-reviews) adds that some jurisdictions may prohibit soliciting reviews, so check your state dental board's rules first.

### The FTC's Fake Review Rule

The FTC's [Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) took effect on October 21, 2024. It bans fake reviews and incentives tied to a positive or negative review, with civil penalties for knowing violations.

### HIPAA in Your Review Replies

A reply that confirms someone is your patient can disclose protected health information, even when the patient posted under their own name. The ADA's guidance warns that even "Thank you for coming in" can violate HIPAA, and suggests a plain "Thank you!" for positive reviews. In 2022 a California practice, New Vision Dental, [paid $23,000 to HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/new-vision/index.html) and accepted a corrective action plan after disclosing patient information in replies to online reviews.

The same rule covers testimonials and before-and-after photos on your own site. In a 2025 settlement, the director of HHS's Office for Civil Rights said a valid, written HIPAA authorization is generally necessary before a provider posts a patient's information in a [website testimonial](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-hipaa-with-cadia-healthcare-facilities.html). Before publishing a story that identifies a patient, get a valid, written HIPAA authorization.

For a negative review, reply in general terms about how the practice works and invite the person to call the office. Never mention a visit, a treatment, a name or insurance.

## Build Treatment Pages and Dentist Bios Google Can Trust

Give every treatment you want patients for its own page: implants, Invisalign, emergency care, pediatric dentistry, crowns, veneers. A patient searching "dental implants Phoenix" wants a page about implants at your office, not a one-line entry in a services list.

A useful treatment page answers what the patient is weighing before they call:

- Who the treatment suits, and who it does not
- What happens at each visit, and how long the whole process takes
- Recovery and aftercare
- What drives the cost, and which insurance plans or financing you accept
- Which dentist does it, with a link to their bio
- How to book, with the phone number and booking link above the fold

Who wrote a treatment page matters. Google's guidance on [helpful, reliable content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) asks whether a page is "written or reviewed by an expert or enthusiast who demonstrably knows the topic well". Put the reviewing dentist's name, credentials and a review date on each treatment page, linked to a bio with their degree, license and specialties. Our guide to [E-E-A-T for AI search](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/eeat-ai-search) covers how those signals carry into AI answers.

If you run several offices, give each location its own page with its address, hours, dentists and the treatments offered there, and link it from that location's Business Profile.

### Keywords, Titles and Patient-Question Articles

Build the page list from the words patients use, as Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) advises: treatment plus city, urgent needs and insurance questions. Give each page a title that names the treatment and the city, and a short meta description unique to that page.

Articles that answer patient questions, such as what to do about a knocked-out tooth, reach potential patients who are still researching. Link each one to the matching treatment page with descriptive link text, so readers and Google both find the page that books the visit.

### Schema for Dental Practices

Schema is code that labels a page's facts for search engines. Mark the practice up with the [Dentist type](https://schema.org/Dentist), a subtype of LocalBusiness and MedicalBusiness, and each bio with Person. Google's LocalBusiness documentation requires only the name and address, and its [review snippet rules](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet) limit local business stars to sites that collect reviews about other businesses, so marking up reviews on your own site will not earn review stars. One older tactic no longer pays either: Google's FAQ rich result [stopped appearing in Search](https://developers.google.com/search/updates#removing-faq-rich-result) on May 7, 2026. Keep the questions on the page for patients. Our [schema guide for AI search](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/schema-markup-for-ai) goes deeper.

## Directories and Best-Of Lists AI Engines Read

Neither replaces your site, but ChatGPT's answers in our test leaned on both.

### Health Directories

Check your profiles on the directories ChatGPT linked (Healthgrades, WebMD's provider directory, Opencare) and on Yelp; AI Mode also linked Zocdoc. Some are paid services: Opencare charges a fee after each patient visit it sends. Keep the name, address, phone, hours and dentists identical everywhere.

### Local Best-Of Lists

Check the lists that rank dentists in your city. dentistsranked.com, the site ChatGPT linked most often in our test, states on its site that its rankings can't be bought and that paid placements are labelled Featured. Read how each list picks practices and correct wrong details where you can. Our [ChatGPT citations guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/chatgpt-citations) explains how ChatGPT picks pages to cite.

### Local Links

Google's local ranking guidance counts how many websites link to your business toward prominence. For a practice, the realistic links are local: community sponsorships, local news, the dental associations you belong to and specialists who refer patients to you.

## Technical Basics for a Dental Website

Make sure each treatment and location page loads fast on a phone, has its own URL and is not blocked from indexing; our [technical SEO guide](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/technical-seo) has the full checklist. Two checks are specific to dental and AI search.

### Tracking Tags on Booking and Patient Pages

Ad and analytics tags on booking pages and patient portals can pass patient information to the tag vendor. HHS [guidance on online tracking technologies](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html) says tags on logged-in pages such as portals generally have access to protected health information, and tags on a public booking page may too. On June 20, 2024, a federal court struck down one part of that guidance: treating an IP address plus a visit to a public page about a condition or provider as protected. HHS says it is evaluating next steps. Review what each tag sends with a qualified HIPAA adviser; a vendor that receives protected health information needs a business associate agreement (BAA), and some tags may need to go.

### AI Crawler Access

If your robots.txt blocks the crawlers AI engines use, those engines may not fetch your pages. Our free [AI crawler checker](https://geotoolbox.ai/tools/ai-crawler-checker) reads your robots.txt and shows which of 34 AI crawlers it allows or blocks.

## Hiring a Dental SEO Agency: Cost, Proof and Ownership

Agency prices for dental SEO run from about $500 to over $5,000 a month. [One agency's pricing guide](https://blog.titanwebagency.com/dental-seo-services-cost) puts basic local work at $500 to $1,200 a month and the most aggressive campaigns at $2,500 to $5,000+. These are sellers' figures. We found no independent survey of what practices pay.

Commenters on r/Dentistry describe paying for a year of SEO with nothing to show for it, and warn about agencies that keep the website and ad accounts when the contract ends. Google's own advice on hiring an SEO says no one can guarantee a #1 ranking.

Own your accounts before anyone starts work. A Business Profile can have several owners but [only one primary owner](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en), and that should be someone at the practice, with the agency added as a manager. The same goes for the domain, the website hosting, Google Analytics, Search Console and any ad accounts. If the agency or its call-tracking and form tools will handle patient information, ask for a BAA: HHS expects one with any vendor that receives protected health information from your site.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is SEO still worth it for dentists now that patients use AI?

Yes, but the work has widened beyond the website: the profile, reviews and directory listings now feed AI answers too.

### Is it worth paying someone for SEO?

It depends on what you can do in-house. The profile, review requests and directory listings take staff time more than skill, so many practices can run them. Treatment pages, technical fixes and measurement are where outside help earns its fee, as long as you keep ownership of every account and judge the agency on booked patients.
